# Scroll height floor — make the transcript's extent monotone by construction {#scroll-height-floor}

A working brief for the interactive sessions that follow the `follow-bottom-clamp` join. It records what is now proven, states the governing assertions, and lays out the work in order. It is deliberately a brief, not a devise plan: the work is interactive and each stage should be designed at the keyboard, against the running app, with the proofs in `tests/app-test/at0336-conservation-probe.test.ts` as the measuring instruments.

## What is proven (2026-08-03) {#proven}

The full evidence chain is in `dash/follow-bottom-clamp.md` under "F1 diagnosed"; the diagnostic suite is `at0336-conservation-probe.test.ts`; the probe surface is `window.__tug.getListConservation(selector)`.

- **The eviction height ledger is exact.** Across 109 eviction events including full-transcript window swaps, the worst ledger-vs-live accounting error is 1.5px spread over 15 rows. Eviction-as-concept is sound; no revert is needed.
- **Every observed displacement — the ~24px turn-boundary dips and the 2,368px window-swap pull — is one mechanism: WebKit clamps the scroll offset synchronously at renderer removal, inside React's mutation phase.** React processes deletions before sibling style updates, so for one unobservable instant the removed cells are gone while the spacers still hold their old heights; WebKit clamps against that transient extent and nothing restores the position when the spacers grow microseconds later. No JavaScript read or write occurs in the gap (proven by wrapping every scroll mover and layout-forcing getter with stack capture), no inter-commit height dip exists (proven by the per-commit geometry ring), and scroll anchoring is not involved (proven by an `overflow-anchor: none` A/B, pixel-identical result).
- **The countermeasure works.** Pinning the scrollable extent with an absolutely-positioned one-pixel height post drives the identical swap sequence to zero displacements. That experiment is the last test in `at0336`.

The consequence for the previous plan: the [S02]/[S03] detect-and-repair machinery was built to undo a displacement that should instead be made impossible. It demotes to an assertion layer once the floor exists.

## Governing assertions {#assertions}

These are the user's cases, restated with the amendments the diagnosis forced. Everything that happens to the transcript's geometry and scroll position must be explainable by an explicit pin to a human-initiated or machine-initiated action; shrinkage is especially suspect because the transcript grows over time.

- **Case A (strengthened).** The transcript's scrollable extent is non-decreasing *at every instant, including mid-mutation* — not merely at observable boundaries, because the browser acts on states no scroll API can witness. The only exceptions are explicitly attributed shrinks: user collapse of a block, pane-width re-wrap, font/density/theme reflow, session clear, data-source swap. Each of those lowers the floor through a declared rebase write, never by letting the content dip out from under it.
- **Case B.** Follow-bottom is engaged unless a direct, attributed user action disengages it (wheel-up, scrollbar drag, keyboard navigation). With Case A enforced this becomes implementable: clamps are impossible and our own writes are counted, so an unattributed upward scroll *is* the user — which closes the scrollbar-drag hole (thumb drags deliver no pointer or wheel events, only scroll events). Amendments: user-requested machine scrolls (find-reveal, focus-reveal) disengage explicitly as part of their command; scroll anchoring is gated off while following, since holding mid-viewport content still is definitionally opposed to the pin.
- **Case C.** Scrolling caused by direct user action is never counteracted. This falls out of A + B once the repair layer is assertion-only; the one residual obligation is ordering — a user disengage must run in the capture phase, synchronously ahead of any pin scheduled in the same frame.

## The work, in order {#work}

**1. Productize the height floor in `TugListView`.** A render-phase element (the height post shape: absolutely positioned, one pixel wide, pointer-events none) whose height ratchets with the ledger's predicted total content height, so the scrollable extent cannot dip mid-mutation regardless of React's mutation order. Design questions to settle interactively: where the floor value comes from (ledger total vs. last observed `scrollHeight`), the rebase paths for each attributed shrink in Case A and how each declares itself (`noteExternalWrite`-style pins), slack policy (a floor briefly taller than settled content leaves scrollable emptiness at the bottom — decide how and when it snaps down, and that snap is itself a declared write), and interaction with `content-visibility` and the width-settle freeze.

**Stage 1 landed (2026-08-03).** The design questions resolved as follows, all on main:

- *Floor value*: the last settled extent, measured in the commit bracket — not the ledger total, which cannot know leading/trailing content or the block-axis pseudo-padding. The floor is `.tug-list-view-floor` (rendered with no height; the bracket is its single writer, [L06]) and is set to `extent − 1` every bracket. The `− 1` keeps the floor from ever defining `scrollHeight`, so `scrollHeight` stays a truthful content measurement; when a shrink makes the floor the thing `scrollHeight` reports, the true extent is recovered from the bottom spacer's bottom edge plus a self-calibrating inset (no `getComputedStyle`, guarded against viewport-defined readings). **Amended 2026-08-03 — the self-calibrating inset is gone.** A calibration refreshed on only some commits can be captured mid-shrink, when the rows have left but the extent has not yet followed them down; the remembered number is then a whole group's height rather than a pad, and every commit after it adds that number back to a correctly measured edge — so the recovery RAISES the floor and the scroller settles at `content + pad`, a fixed point of permanent phantom scroll space under a list that fits. The extent is now measured on every commit as the bottom spacer's bottom edge plus the block-end pseudo-padding read from the element (`trailingPadOf`), which cannot go stale; `scrollHeight` is not an input at all, since the floor contributes to it and would hold itself up. Gated by `tests/app-test/at0337-extent-floor-phantom.test.ts`.
- *Rebase = the same write* (set-to-extent, chosen over strict ratchet + enumerated paths): every bracket sets the floor to the settled extent, up or down, so no shrink path can be forgotten — a shrink nobody anticipated lowers the floor one commit later instead of leaving permanent phantom scroll space. A downward write is the declared rebase: it flushes layout so any clamp happens inside the declared write, pins it via `noteExternalWrite`, and records an always-recorded `extent-rebase` trace event (the Case A attribution pin). Slack is bounded at one commit; session clear, data-source swap, width re-wrap, and `content-visibility` all need no special casing.
- *Results*: the at0335 swap replica that deterministically displaced ~2,368px records **zero** displacements (at0336 5/5, every drive zero); the `forceCommitClamp` simulation drops the floor for its synchronous window so the detector stays testable. Two at0335 opens slated for stage 3 fell out during the work: `priorRepairHeld` null/false (the repair marker is now carried through quiet commits and judged against the baseline, not the new displacement's landing spot) and the 326px idle-recovery shortfall (`maybePinToBottom` now also judges at-bottom against the resting geometry — `lastScrollEventTop`/`lastScrollEventHeight` — because a whole turn landing in one commit made the idle user measure hundreds of pixels "away" from a bottom they never left). **at0335 is 9/9 for the first time.**

**2. Demote the repair machinery to assertions.** With the floor in place, the commit bracket stops repairing and starts asserting: any detected displacement is recorded loudly (deck-trace + dev log) as a bug, never silently counter-written. Keep the bracket, the counters, the registry, and the probe — they are the instruments that proved this diagnosis and they stay.

**Stage 2 landed (2026-08-03).** The bracket is now a pure witness, on main:

- *Assert, never repair*: on an unexplained displacement the bracket counts, records (`scroll-displacement` still bypasses the trace gate), and logs at **warn** — the position is left exactly where the browser put it. `notifyRepair` and `_repairSuppressionArmed` are deleted from `SmartScroll`; the `repaired`/`priorRepairHeld` fields and the whole `pendingRepairTop` carry-forward machinery are deleted with them (whether a displacement persisted is read from consecutive records — the next record's `from` baseline sitting at the previous `to`).
- *Attribution replaces suppression*: the bracket calls `noteExternalWrite()` on detection, syncing SmartScroll's baseline to the clamped position, so the displacement's still-undelivered scroll event arrives with no unexplained delta — the `unattributed-scroll-up` and `drag-up` intent rules never mistake the browser's move for the user, without any one-shot exemption flag. Case B's "an unattributed upward scroll *is* the user" stays sound because the bracket's witness makes the clamp an attributed move.
- *Test contract*: at0335's repair test became "a clamp on a user parked mid-history is witnessed loudly, never counter-written" — the position rests at the record's `to`, follow-bottom keeps the user's state, and the warn-level dev-log record exists with no opt-in. `forceCommitClamp` keeps taking the floor down for its synchronous window; SURFACE_VERSION 1.24.0 records the behavioral change.
- *Results*: at0335 9/9, at0336 5/5 (zero displacement records on every drive), core tier 20/20, scroll set green (at0330's expand/collapse round-trip red is the pre-existing baseline failure, outside this program), tugdeck unit 5530/0, harness 61/0, tsc + vite build clean.

**3. Re-point `at0335` at zero.** The original success criterion — `data-scroll-displacements === "0"` — becomes honest again. Restore it, and fold the two remaining legitimate failures (the 326px idle-recovery shortfall, the `priorRepairHeld` null) into the interactive investigation. Keep `at0336` permanently as the conservation harness.

**Stage 3 landed (2026-08-03).** Zero is the criterion again, on main:

- *Every real drive asserts zero on both surfaces* — `data-scroll-displacements` reads `"0"` and the trace ring holds no records — through a shared `expectZeroDisplacements` helper: the sixty-turn stand-up, the streamed-under-a-parked-user drive, the quiet spell, the repeated window swaps, and the wheel run. The magnitude pins (worst displacement ≤ the at-bottom band) are deleted along with the helper that computed them; a magnitude band would only give a regression room to hide in now that the floor spans the ledger-settle dips that once made ~24px records honest.
- *The `forceCommitClamp` tests stay exempt by design* — they take the floor down and manufacture the record deliberately — and now also pin a zero baseline before arming the clamp, so a defect that displaced before the simulation cannot masquerade as the simulation's record.
- *The two failures this stage was slated to fold in* (the 326px idle-recovery shortfall, the `priorRepairHeld` null) were both discharged during stage 1 — the resting-geometry at-bottom judgment fixed the first, and the second's machinery was deleted outright in stage 2 — so nothing remained to investigate.
- *Results*: at0335 9/9 under the zero criterion, at0336 5/5. `at0336` stays permanently as the conservation harness.

**4. Rebuild follow-bottom on Case B's polarity.** Engaged is the resting state; disengagement requires an attributed action; re-engagement happens on attributed arrival at the bottom. Includes the anchoring gate while following and the capture-phase disengage ordering from Case C. The gesture-end `isAtBottom` re-engage path from the previous plan is implemented but unpinned — pin it here.

**Stage 4 landed (2026-08-03).** Case B's polarity holds by audit and by construction, on main:

- *Disengage-source audit*: every disengage in the tree is a direct user action (`wheel-up`, `key-up`, `drag-up`, `unattributed-scroll-up`, `page-up-key`, `scroll-home-key`) or a user-requested machine scroll declaring itself as part of its command (`find-reveal`, `focus-reveal`, `block-fold`, `diff-view-toggle`, `question-review`, `inline-dialog`, the restore paths `restore-target`/`region-scroll-restore`). One polarity violation found and fixed: the inline-dialog scope's close-side `engage` was unconditional, flipping follow-bottom back on over a user who had scrolled away to read history while the question waited. It now re-engages only on attributed arrival — `Scroller.isSettledAtBottom()` (new façade member, delegating to the extracted `SmartScroll.isSettledAtBottom` getter that also powers `growth-at-bottom-reengage`'s live-OR-resting judgment).
- *Capture-phase ordering (Case C)*: SmartScroll's `wheel` and `pointerdown` listeners moved to `capture: true` on the container, so a user disengage lands synchronously ahead of any descendant handler — including one that stops propagation (the Cmd-wheel inner-scroller route, which now attributes directly instead of falling to the unattributed rule) or synchronously flushes a pinning commit. `keydown` deliberately stays bubble-phase: a key consumed by a descendant control is that control's key, not scroll intent; bubble semantics are exactly that attribution filter.
- *Anchoring gate*: `_setFollowingBottom` stamps `overflow-anchor: none` on the container while following and clears it on disengage — anchoring's hold-content-still is definitionally opposed to the pin, and the disengaged reader (who the reading-position hold exists for) gets it back the moment they park. Applied at construction, cleared at dispose under the registry-ownership guard.
- *Pins*: at0333 grew from 3 to 5 tests — "a wheel nudge at the live edge re-engages when the gesture ends" (the previously-unpinned `gesture-end-at-bottom-reengage`, plus the anchoring gate's `none`/`""` states) and "an inline dialog's close re-engages only a user still at the bottom" (a real AskUserQuestion dialog driven over the wire; the negative assertion — no `inline-dialog` engage after the user scrolled away — is the pin for the polarity fix).
- *Results*: at0333 5/5, at0335 9/9, at0336 5/5; the 17-file scroll + capture-sensitive selection green except two known families — the foreground-tier `document.hasFocus()` timeouts (at0127/at0249/at0250/at0267/at0272, proven environmental by a `tugutil file probe` baseline run with the whole diff reverted) and at0330's pre-existing expand/collapse round-trip red; at0162 flaked once under the serialized run and passes solo. tsc clean on both trees, tugdeck unit 5531/0, vite build clean, covers-check clean.

**5. Amend the doctrine.** `tuglaws/scroll-intent.md` and [D93]: the clamp model is "removal-time, mid-mutation, no reader required," superseding "forced layout in the commit-phase gap." The floor becomes the stated defense; the bracket becomes the stated witness.

**Stage 5 landed (2026-08-03).** The doctrine now says what the code does, on main:

- *The clamp model*: `scroll-intent.md`'s "Window geometry is atomic" became **"The clamp happens at removal time"** — WebKit clamps synchronously at renderer removal inside React's mutation phase, no reader required and no scroll API able to witness it. The superseded model ("the clamp fires when something forces layout in the interval") is named and kept visible rather than deleted, with the three proofs that falsified it (mover/getter stack capture silent in the gap, no inter-commit height dip in the geometry ring, the pixel-identical anchoring-off A/B). The atomic-geometry rule survives it, restated as what it actually buys: not immunity, but an instant-wide dip instead of a commit-wide one.
- *The floor is the stated defense*: a new section states Case A as an invariant and gives the three rules that hold it — set-to-extent rather than a ratchet with enumerated shrink paths (self-healing, slack bounded at one commit), lowering as the declared rebase (`noteExternalWrite` + the always-recorded `extent-rebase` event, which is Case A's attribution pin), and the `− 1` that keeps `scrollHeight` a truthful content measurement. The rebase joins the attribution inventory as its own row.
- *The bracket is the stated witness*: "Repair is bounded to the commit phase" became **"The commit bracket is a witness, never a repairer"** — why the old reasoning was sound and the remedy still wrong, what the bracket keeps (the scroll-event baseline, detection-attributes-the-move, the loud unconditional record, persistence read from consecutive records) and what is gone (`notifyRepair`, the suppression flag, the carry-forward, `repaired` / `priorRepairHeld`).
- *Stage 4's rules got their doctrine*: a new **"Follow-bottom's polarity"** section names the two legitimate kinds of disengage (and declares a third kind a defect), the attributed-arrival condition on a machine scope's close-side re-engage, the capture-phase ordering rule with keydown's deliberate exception, and the anchoring gate. [D93] carries the same three amendments, and its stale "`overflow-anchor` is not used (Safari doesn't support it)" is retired — WebKit honors it, and the reading-position hold depends on it.
- *Also refreshed*: the diagnose section (the `extent-rebase` record and how to read it against a displacement, `getListConservation`, zero as an every-drive invariant rather than a settled-transcript one), the cross-references (at0335's zero criterion, at0336 as the permanent conservation harness, this brief), and `tuglaws/INDEX.md`'s one-line summary.
