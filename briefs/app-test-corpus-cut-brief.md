# The app-test corpus cut: a recorded baseline, and a corpus that guards what Tug is for

**Purpose:** The app-test corpus takes hours to run whole, carries reds nobody owns, spends minutes per file building transcripts so a card has something to move, tests generic browser controls, and repeats itself. Every such file is a tax on development, and the attempt to migrate all of it onto bodies at once stalled the every-card-a-body arc for hours. This brief records the first whole-corpus baseline on main, keeps every finding in it that points at a real product bug, and decides how the corpus is cut down, slice by slice, on main.

---

## Purpose {#purpose}

The user, 2026-10-16:

- "slow tests removed. extend out tests so that none take >20s (I expect a hard 10s limit was too strict)."
- "evaluate tests to remove redundant tests that do basically the same work as other tests."
- "remove obsolete tests that have been overtaken by changes in the way the app works."
- "remove all red tests. there should be no lingering failures in the app-test suite."
- "I think this test suite over-indexes on browser-centric input controls. THIS IS NOT AN ESSENTIAL ASPECT OF TUG." and "Every one of these non-essential app-tests is a TAX on our development efforts."
- "This thing where the test writes in 25 freaking passes of text into a transcript just to move a card must be banished from the app-test suite. It's ridiculous to wait a minute or more for a single test to run."
- Of the body-grounding chunk: "I never ever ever ever again want to run these tests as they are now. They are redundant and take far too long."
- "I want to retain the findings which point to actual bugs."

Two infrastructure complaints rode alongside: the user cannot reliably see a long run's progress in the transcript, and the results ledger looked too thin to answer what is slow or red.

Context: the every-card-a-body arc joined at its step 9 (`6ad15611d`). Its steps 10 and 11 flipped the whole corpus onto bodies at once with a 10 s budget, 324 of 486 files went red together, and that work was retracted to `quarry/every-card-a-body-corpus` with the uncommitted remainder saved as patches in `.tug/arcs/every-card-a-body/`.

## Decisions {#decisions}

**[B01] The corpus guards what Tug is for, and nothing else.** A file stays only if its subject is behaviour a Tug user relies on — sessions, transcripts, the composer as a prompt, cards, arrangement and motion, workspaces, arcs, changes and commits, the host's menus and keys, bodies. Generic input-control behaviour, a reading for a brief, and a guarantee another file already makes are not reasons to keep a file. Every removal names its reason in the commit that removes it.

**[B02] No file takes more than 20 s.** The runner's per-file budget is 20 s, and a file past it is reported red (`OVERTIME`). A file over the line is split along its own `test(` blocks, made cheaper, or deleted; it is never carried. The every-card-a-body arc's 10 s is retired as too strict.

**[B03] Readings leave the corpus.** Benches and readings ([F03] a) move to a directory the sweep and the selector never run (`tests/app-test/readings/`), runnable only by name, with `@covers` kept so a reading can still be found. A reading is an instrument, and its cost belongs to the person who asks for the number. `at0733-set-up-one-layout` goes with them although it asserts a bar (D211: a settle's set-up forces at most one layout), because it takes that bar on the seeded slice and whale transcripts [B04] bans; the bar needs a guard on a bare card inside the budget, rebuilt in the [B04] slice. `zz-probe-layout-miniature` stays: despite its name it is a 10 s test of the Layout card's miniature, green at the baseline.

**[B04] No test seeds a transcript to move a card.** The per-gesture settle family and the motion files built the same way ([F03] b) are deleted. Motion on the real app is read by `tugtool deck motion settle` and the grounding rows. Where a guarantee in a deleted file has no other guard, it comes back as one file on a bare card inside the budget, written against the guarantee, not the old fixture.

Done 2026-10-16, fourteen files: `at0696`–`at0706`, `at0708`, `at0724` and `at0605-still-crossing-deliveries`. The guarantees with no other guard, each to come back on a bare card: a fold's gesture stamp is spent once (`at0696`); a settle dropped or retargeted mid-flight strands no frame at its origin or end pose (`at0698`, `at0699`, `at0701`); a two-commit rail hide carries every frame it moves (`at0700`); a following transcript stays at its bottom on every frame of a split, a stack, and a shrink's land (`at0708`, `at0724`); nothing inside a card is resized while its frame's height tweens (`at0605-still-crossing-deliveries`); and the [D9] guard's rows name `height` and nothing else on a height-bearing gesture (`at0697`). The selector's rule that a file named `atNNNN-settle-…` must carry `@motion` went with the family.

**The transcript files [F03] b also named are not motion files and stay.** `at0330`, `at0333`, `at0335`, `at0336`, `at0387-error-block-follow-bottom`, `at0494`, `at0603`, `at0604` and `at0605-find-adversarial` seed a transcript because their subject is the transcript: eviction, scroll ownership, follow-bottom, and the find walk, which Tug is for. All green at the baseline, all over 20 s (40–144 s). They are taken as over-budget files under [B02], in the slice for the rest over 20 s.

Done 2026-10-16 for those nine: 583 s across nine files became 19 files, every one green alone and under 20 s (4–18 s). Seeding is batched one turn per evaluate (`driveSessionBatch`, 60 turns 4.7 s → 1.7 s; a run spanning several turns' sends stops the app answering). `at0330` is seven files over `eviction-fixture.ts`; `at0335` is `at0335-scroll-clamp` and `at0335-scroll-reader-held`; `at0494` is `-find-reveal`, `-find-reveal-editor` and `-find-paint`; `at0605-find-adversarial` is `-find-burst`, `-find-second-card` and `-find-reveal-range`; `at0333`, `at0387`, `at0603` and `at0604` were cheapened in place. Removed, each with its other guard: `at0336` (a reading; its floor check rides `at0335-scroll-reader-held`), `at0330`'s out-of-view append (`list-view-window-segments.test.ts`), `at0335`'s quiet leg (a 15 s idle with nothing to age) and detect leg (folded into the counter), `at0333`'s disengage and re-engage legs (`at0335` no-revert, the dialog leg), `at0387`'s clean control, `at0494`'s tool-header flash leg (the paint census), `at0604`'s from-the-top walk (CodeMirror reveals its own selection), and `at0605`'s hand-scroll leg (the `revealRange` cancel case). `at0387`'s jitter and flip-record legs moved to `tugdeck/src/__tests__/smart-scroll-jitter.test.ts`, shown red against a disabled shown-extent rule. Several baseline times were batch contention (the two smoke files, `at0554`, `at0580`), several reds were test defects (`chrome-under-bodies` read the Text card in the shell; `at0527` clicked through the restore gate), and the runner's ×4 timeout scale was most of `at0683`, `at0476` and `at0496` ([B08]). `at0555` and `at0566` now move bare cards instead of slice and whale transcripts ([B04]). **[B05 — done 2026-10-16 for the eight single-file reds and for rows 5 and 6.]** Each ran alone once at `7a4815a4f`. Two were batch contention and are green alone: `at0476-arc-interruptions`, `at0522-empty-session-close`. Six held, and every one ended fixed with its test kept as the guard (all eight green alone after the fixes):

- `at0412-text-card-asset-strip` — product. The strip's missing-file probe is sticky by design, and an untitled draft's aside is kept machine-wide (`~/Library/Application Support/Tug/Autosave Information/aside-untitled-<id>.json`) while its asset home is per-instance (`<data_dir>/draft-docs/<id>/assets/`). A restored draft linked files this instance's home never received, the one probe answered 404, and the drop that then brought the file in left the tile broken. Fix: `AssetProjection.notePresent(path)`, called from the drop and paste paths when an upload lands (`file-drop.ts` `onAttached`, `tug-text-card-editor.tsx`). The test mints a draft id per run so a prior run's aside cannot be restored into a fresh instance. The stale `aside-untitled-A.json` on the user's machine was left in place. **Open:** app-tests write real asides and drafts into the user's `~/Library/Application Support/Tug/`, outside the instance dir the harness owns; the harness should redirect them.
- `at0554-session-fold-focus` — product. ⌥⇥ on a folded card set the manual keyboard-focus bit, but the card's cycle scope was pushed only by a React layout effect on the next commit, which a motion gate's hold (the fold still landing) delayed by ~300 ms; a ⇧⇥ in that gap walked the base order (no stops) and was consumed with nothing to show. Fix: `useCycleMode.toggle` enters the cycle in the gesture's own task (`use-cycle-mode.tsx`).
- `at0714-zoom-margin-cap-phase` — product, a WebKit artifact. A 24 px tile is rasterized at the CTM scale and stepped at a whole device pixel, so at 70 % zoom (34 device px per 33.6) the pitch drifts to 24.29 px and the caps fall 3.3 px off the root's lattice by the twelfth line. Fix: each grid axis is one un-tiled `repeating-linear-gradient` over the box, oversized by a tile (`margin-cap.css`, `globals.css`).
- `at0544-resize-sidebars-to-fit` — test. The verb was right (the Overview asks for a fixed 0.75 of the run in `rail-fit.ts`); the Layout card's new Bodies row grew its ask to nearly tie the Overview's, so the sash moved 8.8 px against a 12 px "it moved" margin. The margin is now sub-pixel (3 px).
- `at0648-dictation-finish` — test. A native ⌘D after a chain-only move is adjudicated by AppKit against the last published menu state, which still said the composer held the keyboard, so the menu item fired and the mic opened from a gesture no user can make. The chord is dispatched in-page, as `at0165` does.
- `body-grounding/kind-documents` — test. The settings gear lives in the hover-revealed row, which rests with `pointer-events: none`; the test pins the row open with `revealPaneControls` before clicking, as `at0416` does.

Rows 5 and 6 ran alone at `a76fea5d9`, bodies on, after the cards-and-sessions footing landed; both are green alone after the fixes (row 5 67 s, row 6 143 s):

- `body-grounding/row-05-chords` — test. Its second half read the Text card's editor in the shell, and the Text card has been a body since `6ad15611d`; the leg is now "with the Text card's body holding the keyboard": typing, ⌘Z, ⌘1/⌘3 and ⌃⌘Y from that body, with no other body holding the keyboard meanwhile. The chords themselves were never broken.
- `body-grounding/row-06-menus` — product, in the host. A sidebar shown from View › Sidebar (Arcs, Jots or Layout, a different one each run) left its new body as the window's first responder after the shell had already handed the keyboard back to the card under test. Read on the host with a stack per first-responder change: the last grab came from WebKit's `WebPageProxy::makeFirstResponder` with no Tug frame in it — the new body's page, told it was the key card a beat earlier, focused its stop after the shell had named another card, and WebKit turned that DOM focus into a window-level grab. AppKit consults no `acceptsFirstResponder` on that path, so the gate is `MainWindow.makeFirstResponder`: a body's view takes the keyboard only when the host has named its card (`BodyViewController.keyboardCardId`) or the event being dispatched is a mouse-down inside it; anything else is refused before `super` resigns anyone. This is the daily-deck bug [F04] named: with bodies on, showing a sidebar from the menu could leave the keyboard in the new sidebar while the shell believed the previous card held it.
- `body-grounding/row-07-focus` — the same Text-card-in-the-shell premise as row 5, fixed the same way while it ran as the gate's neighbour. One red leg remains, independent of the gate (reproduced with the refusal switched off): ⌘W from a Session body holding a two-message stub transcript closes the card at once instead of raising the shell's Close popover, although `sessionCloseAdviceFor` confirms whenever the transcript is non-empty; row 6's own notes show the same ("after File › Close: popover false, cards 7 → 6"). A test defect, fixed 2026-10-16 (below): the card held no transcript.

**[B05] A red ends as fixed or deleted, and a red that points at a product bug is not deleted until its bug has a home.** Each of [F04]'s reds is run alone once to tell a defect from a flake. A defect is fixed, with the test kept as its guard; if the fix is not taken now, the finding is written into a brief or an issue in the same commit that deletes the test, so the bug outlives the file. Rows 5 and 6 come first, because they touch the user's daily deck. [F05]'s motion reds go with [B04], after one reading of the same gestures on the real app through the settle verb, so a real motion regression is not lost with its test.

**[B06] Redundant, obsolete and browser-control files are cut by a reading the user approves.** Starting from the triage on the quarry branch ([F07]), the fast green files are read by subject and grouped by guarantee; one file per guarantee is kept, obsolete premises are deleted, and generic control files go ([F06]). The user reads each list before anything is removed.

**[B07] The work lands on main in slices, each one a commit the user lands.** A slice is a list of files with a reason per file, the edits or deletions, and only the cheap checks (`bunx tsc --noEmit` in `tests/app-test`, `just app-test-covers-check`); a slice that changes a file's behaviour runs that file alone as its verdict. No arc, no worktree, no whole-corpus run between slices. The order: readings out ([B03]); transcript-seeding motion files out ([B04]); the product reds ([B05]); the rest over 20 s ([B02]); redundant, obsolete and control files ([B06]); the runner ([B08]); then the bodies migration ([B09]).

**[B08] The runner shows progress and keeps the record.** Multi-file runs print the per-file progress lines and the `Failures:` section and leave `Diagnostics:` to `TUG_APPTEST_JSON` or a one-file run, so a block's result is never truncated before its verdict. No run is ever piped through a filter. A run expected to pass ten minutes is split into chunks that each fit, and the chunk count is said in prose before each one. The full-corpus recipe always records under a named selection, so a baseline can be found again. The runner's timeout scale follows the files actually sharing the machine: `scripts/app-test.sh` clamps its concurrency to the background queue's size, so a one-file run no longer scales every wait ×4 (found 2026-10-16, when a 6 s negative window cost 24 s and one missed retry was 80 of `at0683`'s 85 s; fixed the same day).

**[B08b] A widened subtree is re-keyed, never raised in place (the user, 2026-10-16).** `list-view-reveal-drift.ts` and `find-reveal.ts`, the two pure helpers the find fixes split out, widened `tugdeck/src/components/tugways/internal/` (39 → 40) and `tugdeck/src/components/tugways/tug-text-card-editor/` (6 → 7). The ratchet's own route is taken: one landing deletes both `ACCEPTED_SUBTREES` entries, the next re-adds them at 40 and 7 with the argument, and the check reports them unrecorded between the two. Done: deleted in `d51ef224b`, re-added the next landing; the covers check is green.

**[B08a] Selection counts families, not files, and never trades coverage for a cap.** Splitting a slow file into small ones must not shrink what a change selects, and the fan-out check must not be quieted by raising `ACCEPTED_FANOUT` (the user, 2026-10-16: "I explicitly don't want this. The tests and coverage checks must become smarter"). A split file's pieces each name every source they exercise, as the whole file did, and declare one `@family`; the fan-out lint and the selection cut count a family once (the user chose families over recorded seconds, which CI has no ledger to read). The lint holds a family to its name: members are named `<family>-…` and number at least two. Done 2026-10-16: `select-tests.ts` counts families, `scripts/select-tests-family.test.ts` guards it, and the transcript split's four families (`at0330-eviction`, `at0335-scroll`, `at0494-find`, `at0605-find`) carry their originals' hub `@covers` again.

**[B09] The bodies migration follows the cut, in batches.** What remains after [B01]–[B06] moves onto bodies in batches of ten to fifteen files grouped by card kind, each batch opting in with the launch's `bodies` option and run alone as its verdict, fixes landing in the same commit. The harness default flips to bodies in one line once no file is left un-opted. The quarry branch and patches ([F07]) are mined, never merged.

**[B10] The baseline is the reference.** Every slice cites a file's baseline result (selection `baseline`) when it removes or changes it, and the record's before is that run, not memory.

## Open Questions {#open-questions}

- **What makes the Session card's live Bash block flash and empty mid-run?** [F09]; the user saw it twice during the baseline. A defect in the card, to be read separately from this cut.
- **Does a find reveal into a Read block's editor cancel itself?** Fixed 2026-10-16; two defects stacked. `FileBlock`'s scroll-into-view wrote the transcript scroller undeclared, so the reveal read it as the user's scroll: it now declares the write (`noteExternalWrite`), and the reveal re-bases its drift baseline onto declared writes (`list-view-reveal-drift.ts`, unit-tested). Under that, the reveal's wake-up (`afterActiveEditorMeasure`) ran the repaint inside CM6's measure `read`, where the editor's match selection is a refused transaction: `paint` threw after dropping `activeEditorKey`, every later rect read was null, and the reveal ran to `failed:deadline` over a match in view. The callback now runs in a microtask after the measure, and the transcript asks for it once per reveal (asking from every advance looped). `at0494-find-reveal-editor` asserts `landed` (65 ms); the other six find files are green.
- **Should a Text-card find centre a clipped top-line match?** Fixed 2026-10-16: `selectMatchFromAnchorFn` treated any match not wholly inside the scrollport as off screen and centred it; `findRevealFor` (`tug-text-card-editor/find-reveal.ts`, unit-tested) leaves a wholly visible match alone, nudges a partly visible one to `nearest`, and centres only a match with no part on screen, and the anchor is the first visible row rather than its logical line. `at0604`'s typing leg is back on the dense fixture, red before (215 px jump) and green after (19 px nudge).
- **Why does a Session body with a transcript close without asking?** It does not; it never held one. Row 7's ⌘W leg and row 6's File › Close note, 2026-10-16 at `a76fea5d9`. Read in the app the same day: the body's session store held `transcript.length` 0, phase idle, and the body and the shell both answered `waive: true`, which is the rule (`session-card-close-advice.ts`) applied correctly to an empty card. A stub session plays its transcript only when a prompt is sent into the card (`streaming-stub-fixture.ts`), and `spawnStub` sends none, so every Session card the grounding rows seat is bound and empty; `_bodies.ts` claimed "the stub's turn run out" and now says so plainly. A test defect, fixed: row 7 types an unsent draft into A before ⌘W (a draft always confirms) and now asserts the popover on File › Close too, where it had accepted either outcome; green alone, 9 s.
