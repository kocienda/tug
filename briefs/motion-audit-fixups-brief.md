# Motion audit fixups: close the eight weaknesses, and make the release deck measurable on demand

**Purpose:** The 2026-10-06 audit of the graphics and animation work found the architecture sound and the codebase short of it in eight named places, plus one defect on `main` that no check could see. This brief gives each its ending, and records the user's ruling on the instruments: off by default, switchable within a session.

---

## Purpose {#purpose}

The user asked, after `column-pin-at-the-set-up` joined at `2af615b1d`:

> Let's audit the implementation, as well as all the work that has led up to this point. Where are we? I still want to nail the asks in `briefs/graphics-animations-asks.md`. … Do we need to make fixups? Or, can we move on from here with confidence that the codebase is in good shape?

The audit's answer was fixups first, a bounded list, then move on. The user ruled: "Write a brief to address all eight weaknesses you've defined." And on the one question the audit left to them, whether the motion instruments should run in the product by default:

> Not on by default, but make it possible to switch on the instruments in a session on and off so we can measure and debug the release build.

The workspace switch and the session body's first frame are the two user-visible asks still unbuilt; each already has its own brief (`briefs/workspace-switch-motion-brief.md`, `briefs/session-body-first-frame-brief.md`) and neither is this one's.

## Decisions {#decisions}

**[B01] The restored calls land, and tugdeck gets a linter that refuses a bare expression.** The four-site fix from [F01] is committed as it stands. `just lint` gains a TypeScript linter over `tugdeck/src` with, at minimum, `no-unused-expressions` and `no-unreachable` enabled; whichever tool (eslint with typescript-eslint, biome, oxlint) carries those rules with the least configuration is the one, and its config lives beside `audit-motion.ts`'s invocation so `just lint` runs both. A linter is the only guard that could have seen [F01]: the type checker is satisfied by an expression, the audits read motion, and the app-tests cover the settle's completion exits and not its sweep or unmount. The first run will surface pre-existing hits across tugdeck; each is fixed or carved out by rule in that same change, never left red.

**[B02] A settle's crossings are owned by one ledger, and a carried frame ends what its carrier does not adopt.** `settle-engine.ts` gains a per-settle crossing record — the open marks by frame, with the kind (`still`, `fold`, `settled`) and the id each was opened under — written by every opener (`arm`'s predict loop, the Last pass's adopt/settle/re-mark, the covered branches) and read by one `carry(frame, kinds)` that ends every mark on the frame the carrier did not name, and one `endAll()` that every exit calls: the chain's completion, the no-chain path, the sweep, the unmount, the no-First-rects return and the declining return. `carriedStill`, `retargetedFramesRef` and `armHeldRef` fold into it. This is the structural answer to [F02]: the revealed member's fold, the held member's fold and the sweep's stripped calls were each a frame carried without its other marks being ended, and a `carry` that ends by construction leaves nothing for a reader to remember. `at0689` and `at0698` grow legs for the sweep and unmount exits, since [F01] shows those are the exits no test reads.

**[B03] The gate is the one hold; the session hold goes.** `holdSessions`/`releaseSessions` and `CodeSessionStore.holdNotifications`' use by the engine are removed, with the store's hold API deleted if the engine was its last caller. The gate already holds every React tell those notifies produce, so the second hold buys nothing and costs a second cap, a second release path and a second thing to reason about on every exit. The two quiets stay data-driven as [D7] requires: the arrival's quiet is a fact about the card's content being ready, not about React, and the switch's epoch is `briefs/workspace-switch-motion-brief.md` [B04]'s to fold into the gate when the switch takes the shape. One hold, two declared quiet sources, no third clock.

**[B04] The set-up is shrunk where cheap, as [B07] of the motion brief said, and read on the user's deck.** Two cuts, read with `tugtool deck motion settle --tasks --chains` before and after: the pane chrome stops re-rendering under an arrangement change (a frame's placement, column membership and stack state move the frame, not its title bar, buttons and poppers — memoize the chrome below `TugPaneImpl` against the props that actually change), and the canvas's layout effect takes one layout rather than a chain (`_placeRunHeight`/`getColumnRunHeight` and `_flowBandEdges` read after one forced layout, not several). The lead stays a reading and never a bar, by the user's standing rule; the number that decides is the first-beat start on split, slot, rails, sidebar and fit on the user's deck, beside H4's. Nothing here may move work from the set-up into the motion; the sealed-motion clause holds on every leg throughout.

**[B05] The four reds close by the three endings.** Each has one:

- `at0686`: the pane is grabbed while sliding and never reads pointer-owned. The ending is read, not guessed: run it alone with the sampler's notes, decide whether the drag's threshold is reached under the set-up-and-go slide (the bar may now stand at First for the lead's length, so the native drag's `from` read 60 ms after dispatch lands before the slide moves, and the press catches a frame the settle has not launched) or whether the take (`lib/settle-take.ts`) no longer marks the frame. A product defect is fixed in `tug-pane.tsx`; a test whose premise moved with the shape is changed to read the grab relative to the slide's first frame, as `at0701` was changed to issue its close relative to the arrival's beat.
- `at0706`: the sidebars-show land reads 25–32 ms with nothing the motion lets in. Read what the land frame does by site; if it is the re-shown rail's own first layout, that is set-up work that belongs under the arrival's prepare beat ([B05] of set-up-and-go-fixups treated appear the same way); if the frame is genuinely one period plus jitter, the bar is re-budgeted on the user's word with the reading recorded, as resize-to-fit's was.
- `at0626`: read its failure; the sash drag's lag instrument predates the set-up-and-go shape and may be reading the hold the drag now pays. Fix the drag or change what the test reads, and say which.
- `at0643`: the switch brief's arc owns it ([B04] there puts the switch under the gate and the land row). Until that arc runs, the test's flaky clause is named in that brief rather than carried here.

None is carried. A red that cannot be fixed within reach is a question for the user about the clause, asked in the arc.

**[B06] The streaming floor is read, not fixed, here.** `tugtool deck motion walk` on the user's deck with the Overview's unseen rows skipped and with the Overview removed, and `rest` taken at rest with no session running, go into `briefs/real-transcript-motion-readings.md` beside [F06]'s mid-turn reading. The reading decides whether the Overview's size is the floor; the product call on it is the user's and is named in Open Questions. What this brief does decide is that the reading is taken before any arc claims the slide drops frames for a reason inside the engine.

**[B07] A held delivery is released with its observer.** `holdDeliveries` returns, beside the wrapped callback, a release the wrapped observer's `disconnect` calls, dropping the kept entries and the queued hand-over; the two constructor wrappers in `gesture-scope.ts` wire it, and `heldResizeObserver` does the same. The shape is `wrapSubscribe`'s `forget` ([L27]: every acquisition returns its release). A unit test in `tugdeck/src/lib/__tests__/gesture-scope.test.ts` holds a delivery, disconnects, releases the gate and asserts the callback never ran.

**[B08] Main-thread motion outside the settle is inventoried, each site given its form, and the frame pump goes.** The census in [F08] is the list. Each site is read and gets one of three endings, recorded in the code: it moves to transform/opacity (a width or height tween that can ride a scale or a clip under a hold, the way the settle's width does); it is declared as main-thread by design with its cost read on the user's deck (a Radix accordion's height, a one-shot flash); or it is removed. `wave-caret.ts`'s `requestAnimationFrame` pump is the one decided here: a follow written every frame is [L13]'s "rAF is not for animation" and [D7]'s polling of a push system in one. The follow rides the document update's measure/write phase, as `session-dot-layer.tsx` already does for the dot, and stops with the data. `audit-motion.ts` gains a rule that reports a TugAnimator tween or a `transition` on a layout property outside the carve-out list, so the inventory cannot silently grow.

**[B09] The two standing D9 hits are recorded as a decision, not a gap.** `tuglaws/animation-doctrine.md` [D9]'s paragraph on the hits gains the finding: height by translation was benched twice, the second time on real transcripts and the user's deck, and lost (`briefs/set-up-and-go-motion-brief.md`, Non-goals), so a height term and an over-cap width term cross main-thread for the frame's own box, with the still crossing keeping the subtree out of it, by decision. The sentence "closing it means giving the division a form the compositor can run, which is its own work" becomes "was tried and declined, and is reopened only with a new shape for the division, not a third bench of the same one". `at0697`'s assertion that the rows name `height` and nothing else stays as the fence.

**[B10] The instruments are off by default and switch on and off within a session, on the release build.** The user's ruling. One switch, reachable two ways on a running instance: `tugtool deck motion record on|off` over the existing `diag/eval` door, and a Session card slash command of the same meaning for the deck the user is looking at. `on` enables the recording kinds the settle and the switch write (`settle-frames`, `settle-land`, `settle-beat`, `space-switch-frames`) through `deckTrace.enableKind`, arms the lead recorder and the commit census the same way `__tugTestMode` does today (so a reload is not needed: the `index.html` installers take the flag at evaluation, and the switch sets the sessionStorage flag for the next load as well as arming what it can live), and reports what it armed; `off` disarms all of it and leaves the deck as it was, with every row already recorded still readable. `on` is never persisted across instances and never set by anything but the hand. With recording on, `tugtool deck motion settle` and `slide` read the engine's own rows (the sealed-motion census, the land row, the beat rows) rather than only their outside recorder, and print a verdict line against the fixture's bars — the gap, land, sealed-motion and off-curve clauses of `expectB09Bar`, with the lead noted — so "sometimes drops frames" on the user's deck becomes a named row with a verdict, taken by typing one verb. The three unused bar helpers in the fixture ([F10]) are called by the tests they were written for: `at0697` or a flow-slide leg takes `expectBar`, `at0696` takes `expectFoldBar`, `at0708` takes `expectColumnBar`; a bar that is red on `main` is read and ended by the three endings, not left uncalled.

**[B11] The order is [B01], then [B02], [B07] and [B03], then [B10], then [B05], then [B08], [B04] and [B09], with [B06]'s reading taken as soon as the user's deck is quiet.** The linter goes first because every later change is read through it; the crossing ledger before the reds because `at0686` and `at0706` are read through a settle that must be trusted; the instruments before the reds because [B05]'s endings are decided from readings the switch makes repeatable on the user's deck; the set-up work last because it is performance behind the hard bars and every earlier item makes its readings cleaner.

## Open Questions {#open-questions}

- **Is the Overview the floor?** [B06] takes the reading. If the walk prices the Overview's unseen rows at most of the per-frame cost, whether the card skips them when unseen or holds fewer posts is a product call about the Overview, and the user's; the readings go beside the question and this brief does not pre-empt it.
- **Does a 160 ms lead on rails read as a delay?** The user's rule makes the lead a budget. [B04]'s cuts are expected to bring rails under 100 ms on the user's deck; whether what is left reads as a response or a delay is the user's reading on their deck, not a harness number.
