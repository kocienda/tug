# Workspace switch and create: a parked workspace is a finished picture

**Purpose:** Switching between workspaces and creating a new one are still flashy. The switch is already one cut and the create already lands on faces, and both judges are green; what still flashes is everything that redraws a card *after* the cut, each card on its own clock.

---

## Purpose {#purpose}

The user's report, 2026-11-03: "Switching between workspaces and creating new workspaces *is still far too flashy*. This is an essential feature, and it has not yet reached the level of polish I require. Banish the flashing!"

The earlier rounds (`briefs/switch-hand-over-brief.md`, `briefs/workspace-create-flash-brief.md`) made the switch a single host transaction and the create a cut onto faces, and `body-grounding/row-44-*` and `row-10-*-dormant` hold those. Their bars are "no hole, no card in the wrong place". They say nothing about a card that is in the right place and then changes its colour, blanks its content, or pops in beside a neighbour that popped a frame earlier. That cascade is what is left, and it is what this brief is about.

## Decisions {#decisions}

**[B01] A parked workspace is a finished picture.** What is hidden keeps its own look, its theme, its focus and its content, so the switch shows it rather than remaking it; and what has to load, loads as one. This is the rule the rest derive from, and it is the row for `tuglaws/animation-doctrine.md` D2 and D2a to cite.

**[B02] A body wears its own workspace's theme, always.** The theme a body applies becomes a per-card fact projected from the card's own workspace, the way `paneChromeOf` already resolves `spaceOf(cardId)`, in place of the on-screen theme in `readDeckFacts`. A parked body then never restyles when another workspace's theme is on screen, and the cut's first frame is already in the right tint. A card moved to another workspace restyles then, in the hidden layer. The shell's own flip in the swap commit, and the stand-in and face drawn in the shell's theme, are untouched: they already agree with the arriving cards. Closes [F01], and it is the smallest change with the largest effect on this user's deck.

  *As built:* the theme is a card-scoped shell store, `card.theme`, set from `cardThemeOf` in `tugdeck/src/lib/body-shell.ts` (the card's workspace's recorded theme, or the screen's for a workspace that names none) at seed, on every deck and spaces change, and on an on-screen flip, which moves it only where the workspace names no theme. `deck.facts` keeps its `theme`, because the overlay page reads it and the overlay wears the screen's theme; a body reads that field only from the host's bind facts, for its first paint, and follows `card.theme` from then on. On `row-44-switch-theme` the arriving cards' content holds its colour from the first switched frame.

**[B03] No lid on a dormant reveal.** A revealed dormant body shows what it had, and the catch-up lands in place as any live card takes a frame. The content lid stays only for a page that has never painted (`data-content-painted` absent). This reverses the stage-one reading in [F03], in the open: the mid-turn case is re-read with the fix, and if a transcript landing mid-turn moves something a reader sees, that is the list view's landing to fix, not a reason to blank the card. The user agreed to this on 2026-11-03. Closes [F02].

  *As built:* the dormant lid is gone: dormancy no longer touches the content box, `data-body-dormant` no longer exists, and the one content lid left is the never-painted page's. The judges read the lid from the content box's own `::after` style rather than from an attribute, so they see either rule. *The mid-turn case re-read with the fix* (`row-35-dormancy`, a Session body that slept while its turn streamed): the revealed card is on screen 12 ms after the switch showing the transcript it had, mid-stream, and its catch-up lands at 125 ms, so it shows its old content for 113 ms, about 6.8 display periods, with no reading lidded. That is the stage-one reading's 4–6 periods again, now spent showing content rather than a blank box. Whether that landing moves anything a reader sees is the transcript list view's to make still; nothing here blanks the card for it. `row-10-workspace-session-home-dormant` and `-away-dormant` are green on the blank-box bar, every arriving page drawing 90–92 frames from its reveal with none lidded.

**[B04] Faced cards reveal as a set, under a horizon.** When a placement faces several cards of one switch, the host keeps every face standing until all of them have content-painted, then swaps faces for pages in one transaction. A horizon of about two seconds, counted from the switch, releases the hold: past it, painted cards show as they come, so a page that never paints holds nothing for ever ([L33]). The set waits on content, not chrome, so a card never pops twice. The faces are loading cards already, and the user has said loading that reads as loading is fine; the cascade is what is not. The number is the arc's to pick from the lane's recorded times and record. Closes [F04]; the mechanism is [F06]'s.

  *As built:* the faces still retire to the shell's stand-ins at the snapshot as before ([F06]); what the set holds is each member's *paint*. A hand-over that faces two or more cards whose content has not painted opens a set (`BodyRevealSet`, `tugapp/Sources/BodyHandOver.swift`); the controller holds a member's paint — not shown, not reported to the shell, its eviction cover kept — until every member's content has painted, then lands them all in one `CATransaction`, so the shell hears every member painted together and drops its stand-ins in one frame. A later hand-over of the same switch moves the set onto its faces rather than closing it. *The set waits on content:* a page posts `painted` twice, its chrome's frame and then its content's, and the host could not tell them apart, so a member held on its chrome paint was shown by its content paint as a stranger. `painted` now carries `content`, and only a content paint completes a member. *The horizon is 2000 ms* from the hand-over, picked from the lane's recorded first paints (eight pages by 1.45 s on the user's deck, a create's five rail cards by 1.6 s); `_row-44.ts`'s `SET_HORIZON_MS` matches it, and the judge now reads the host's layers to just past the horizon so a set landing late in it is read landing. Readings: create's five pages show in one frame at 1283 ms (1649 ms in another run), first visit's two at 563 ms, evicted's two at 578 ms; the host's `handOvers` account carries a `revealLog` that row 44 notes.

**[B05] Focus rides the swap commit, and a parked body keeps its focused look.** `activateCard(focus)` moves inside the swap's `_flipFirstResponder` so the first notified arrangement names the focused card, and the shell stops telling a parked card it lost focus at park: the workspace record already remembers `focusedCardId`, and the body's `data-focused` holds through the park. The arriving focused card is lit in the first frame. Closes [F05].

  *As built:* the arriving focus is resolved before the swap and raised inside its one flip, so the first notified arrangement names it and the card hears its activation from that flip; a focus on a parked rail still goes through `activateCard` after the swap, since showing the rail is a commit of its own. A body's focused look is no longer derived from the keyboard's first responder: `card.context` carries `focused`, whether the card is its own workspace's focused card, read off its own deck (the parked record keeps the active pane), and the body draws `data-focused` from that. Its activation notices still read `firstResponderCardId`, so a parked card's keyboard behaviour is unchanged and only its look holds through the park. At this step `row-44-switch-evicted` also reads red on the set-swap bar (faced pages at 238 and 454 ms), and equally with this step reversed: its cold page loads cascade as the create's do, so [B04]'s set reveal owns it with the others.

  *Revised at the set reveal:* the swap's flip goes from the outgoing card to nothing, as it always did, with the arriving focus raised inside its commit and made the responder chain's key after it, so the arriving card hears no activation of its own. Flipping to the arriving card fired its activation fan-out in the cut's task, and that delayed the host's placement past the shell's cut frame: `row-44-switch-first-visit` showed the canvas through both panes at 102–116 ms in two of four runs with it (faces standing at 104–144 ms), and in none of four with the whole arc reversed (faces at 73–76 ms) or with this flip back (72–89 ms). The old code never sent that notice either: its follow-up `activateCard` was a same-card refresh, because the parked record keeps the active pane.

**[B06] A reading first, and judges that fail on today's build.** Four frame recordings in the harness, shaped like row 44's burst: a switch between two awake workspaces wearing different themes; a return to dormant session cards; a first visit after launch; a create. Then three bars added to the row-44 and row-10 judges, each run red against the current build before its fix: after the first switched frame, no pane's content region changes its dominant colour; no content box goes surface-blank on a reveal of a painted body; and a switch's faced set swaps to pages in one frame, or past the horizon. The recordings also answer [F07]. Every new test carries `@covers` and stays under the corpus's 20 s budget.

  *Resolved at the first step:* three of the four recordings already existed as files of the shape asked for, so they carry the new bars rather than being duplicated (the corpus takes no redundant file). The theme switch is the one new file, `row-44-switch-theme`; the return to dormant session cards is `row-10-workspace-session-{home,away}-dormant`; the first visit is `row-44-switch-first-visit`; the create is `row-44-switch-create`. The set-swap bar is on for every gesture row 44's judge reads. The colour bar and the blank-box bar are asked for by the theme file (`holdsColour`, `revealsPainted`), and the blank-box bar holds every row-10 switch onto a body that had painted (every tier but `evicted`). On the dormant reveal it reads the page's own record of its lid at each animation frame beside the capture's pixels, because the harness's lid stands for a frame or two and a 16 ms burst can fall between them. The set-swap bar times each faced card's *page* first on screen, not its face coming down: a face can come down to the shell's stand-in together with its neighbours while the pages behind it cascade, which is the pop-in this bar exists to catch (one reading had all five create faces down at 194 ms and the pages first shown at 461 to 1027 ms). Reds recorded at the first step: `row-44-switch-theme` (colour), `row-44-switch-create` (set swap: pages first shown at 760, 1076, 1143 and 1360 ms), `row-44-switch-first-visit` (set swap: two pages 33 ms apart), and `row-10-workspace-session-home-dormant` and `-away-dormant` (blank box). `row-44-switch-evicted` and `row-38-evicted` are green on it: their faced pages show in one frame. Run in a batch, `row-44-switch-create` also showed the canvas through its rail at its first switched frame, about 100 ms, before the faces stood; alone, and under the base judge, it does not, so that is contention, not a red.

**[B07] The doctrine rows say so.** `tuglaws/animation-doctrine.md` rows D2 and D2a cite this brief beside the two they already cite, with [B01] as the target in words.

## Open Questions {#open-questions}

- **What the first frame after an unhide shows ([F07]).** If the recording in [B06] finds a frame of nothing between the unhide and the view's layer tree, the fix is the host's: hide a parked body by opacity or by a cover, not `isHidden`, and the eviction and dormancy clocks keep reading `shown` as they do now ([B04] of the fixups brief). If the frame is clean, nothing changes. The recording settles it either way.

  **Answered 2026-11-03: the frame is clean, so nothing changes.** In every recording the first frame after the host unhid a parked view shows that view's last layer tree and nothing else: no bare window and no canvas in the box (`row-44-switch-theme`: both cards at 25 ms, box bare 0.0%, canvas no more than settled; `row-10-workspace-session-*-dormant` and `-kinds-*-dormant`: the first shown frame at 34–103 ms, no frame of 37 showing a bare hole). What that layer tree *holds* is the fault, never its absence: a parked awake body's first frame wears the departed workspace's tint (`rgb(57, 59, 62)` where the light workspace settles at `rgb(249, 250, 251)`, changing at 41 ms and again later), and a dormant body's first frame is its lid (content ink 0.12% against a settled 1.15%; its page draws one or two frames under the lid, 12–20 ms after the reveal, on the harness's stub, whose catch-up closes at once). So the host's `isHidden` stays, and [B03] and [B02] are the fixes.

## Non-goals {#non-goals}

- **Load time, the lane's width, or a spare per rail card.** How long a page takes to paint is not the subject; [B04] holds the reveal rather than hurrying it. Rejected once already in `briefs/workspace-create-flash-brief.md`.
- **A progress glyph on the lid or the face.** Removed at the user's direction on 2026-10-27 (`e6c94ec2b`): a host-drawn face cannot animate it, and a still glyph read as stuck.
- **Keeping hidden bodies awake to avoid the catch-up.** Dormancy and eviction stay as they are; [B03] changes what a reveal shows, not when a body sleeps.
- **Pre-tinting bodies to the on-screen theme ahead of a switch.** Considered and rejected: it is the current mechanism's premise, and it is what puts the departed tint on the arriving cards. A body's theme is a fact of its workspace, not of the screen.
- **Warming a workspace's pages before showing it.** Rejected in the create brief and stays rejected — except for New Workspace through the naming modal, which `briefs/workspace-naming-modal-brief.md` [B10] carves out: the modal answers the gesture at once, so the loading behind it makes nobody wait on an empty screen.

## Exit {#exit}

An arc. The first act is [B06]'s four recordings on the current build, which settle [F07] and give each judge its red. Then [B02], the per-card theme, and [B05], focus in the swap commit, which are shell changes and close the two flashes every switch pays. Then [B03], the reveal without its lid, with the mid-turn case re-read. Then [B04], the set reveal in the host, which is the largest piece and the one the create needs. [B07]'s doctrine rows land with the last fix.
