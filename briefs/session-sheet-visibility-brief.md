# Sheets on a folded card and in a short pane: fully visible, wherever the card stands

**Purpose:** A sheet raised on a Session card that is folded, or that stands in a pane too short to hold the panel, is clipped by its own pane or painted over by a neighbouring one. The card's surfaces must be fully visible in every one of those forms.

---

## Purpose {#purpose}

In the user's words:

> When we minimize (i.e. fold) a session card or display one in a low-height split, we *still must make* Z2 popups fully visible when I click. Also, session sheets must also be fully visible in all cases in these circumstances. Right now, they're clipped and/or hidden. We may need to portal them. Must have a representation that works in this mode.

Two screenshots came with it. In the first, the AI settings sheet — the one the Z2 `AI MODEL SETTINGS` chip opens — is up on a card whose composer holds a long prompt; the panel's top is cut off under the masthead, so the first model rows are lost. In the second, a folded card is compacting and its `Compacting` cover drops below the folded frame and is cut off by the top edge of the card beneath it.

The "Z2 popups" the user clicks on are those chips' sheets. The Z2 telemetry placards (STATE, TIME, TOKENS, TASKS, JOBS) are a different surface class and are not the defect ([F08]).

---

## Evidence {#evidence}

**[F01] Every Session card sheet portals into its pane's frame and is clipped there.** `TugSheetContent` renders through `createPortal` into the element from `TugPaneFrameContext` (`tugdeck/src/components/tugways/tug-sheet.tsx:878`, `:1801`). The portal's one child is `.tug-sheet-clip`: `position: absolute`, `top` at the chrome's bottom edge, `overflow: hidden`, `z-index: 2` *inside the pane frame's stacking context* (`tug-sheet.css:77–90`). The doctrine is `tuglaws/pane-model.md:465`: a pane-modal surface "paints inside the Pane's stacking context, so peer Panes z-stacked above paint above the modal panel automatically." The CSS comment on the clip says the same in fewer words: where the clip extends past the chrome, the panel paints "under a higher-z peer pane otherwise — both correct stacking behavior for an in-pane modal." **(verified)**

**[F02] Pane z-order is focus order, so an auto-raised sheet on an unfocused pane loses to whichever pane the user is working in.** `deck-canvas.tsx:1241–1260` assigns each pane `CARD_ZINDEX_BASE + i` from its position in the store's array, which is focus order. The `Compacting` cover is raised by `session-compaction-run.tsx:224` from a store watcher, not a click, so the folded card it belongs to is typically *not* the focused pane. Its clip is `100vh` tall (top-anchored, `tug-sheet.css:89`) and the canvas clamp sizes the panel to the canvas bottom, so the panel is the right size — and the focused card below paints over it. That is the second screenshot exactly, and it is the doctrine in [F01] doing what it says. **(verified in code; the screenshot is the reproduction)**

**[F03] A bottom-anchored sheet is capped at its own frame's bottom edge, and a short pane cannot hold it.** Sheets opened from Z2 take `bottomAnchorSelector: MODAL_REST_LINE` with `presentation: "rise"` (`ai-config-sheet.tsx:250–251`; the rest line is `.session-view-slot`, `cards/modal-rest-line.ts`). The bottom-anchor effect (`tug-sheet.tsx:1088–1155`) measures the band between the chrome and the rest line; when the panel needs more, it slides the clip's bottom edge down "stopping `SHEET_CANVAS_GAP` above the frame's bottom edge" — the `floor` at `:1128` is computed from `frame.bottom`, never from the canvas. So on a pane whose whole height is less than the panel plus its gutters, the clip is the pane, `max-height: calc(100% - 2 * space-a)` should cap the panel to it, and the panel is either scrolled or — as the first screenshot shows — overflows the clip's top edge and is cut. **(the floor is verified; *why* the cap fails to bite in the screenshot rather than producing a scrolled panel is inference — a flex column with `justify-content: flex-end` whose child's minimum content height exceeds the container overflows at the top, and the sheet's inner body is an `overflow: hidden` column at `tug-sheet.css:262–286` that may be reporting a min-content height above the cap. One DevTools read of the panel's computed height against the clip's confirms or redirects.)**

**[F04] On a folded card the rest line has no box, and the anchor arithmetic runs on zeros.** At rest a folded card writes `data-fold="settled"` and `session-card.css:1584` takes the view slot out of layout: `.session-card[data-fold="settled"] .session-view-slot { display: none; }`. `getBoundingClientRect()` on a `display: none` element is all zeros, so at `tug-sheet.tsx:1094` `restInset = frame.bottom - 0`, which places the clip's bottom edge at the top of the viewport, and the shortfall branch then drives it to the `floor` in [F03] — the folded frame's own bottom, 144px below the masthead (`SESSION_MINIMIZED_HEIGHT_PX`, `session-card-registration.tsx:40`). Every rest-line sheet on a folded card is therefore told to fit inside a 144px card. The `Compacting` cover escapes this only because it is one of the top-anchor exemptions in `modal-rest-line.md`. **(verified by reading; not yet caught with a DevTools measurement)**

**[F05] The frame itself does not clip; only the chrome does.** `.tug-pane-chrome` has `overflow: clip` and `isolation: isolate` (`tug-pane.css:201`, `:227`); the outer `.tug-pane` frame has neither, and `tug-pane.css:1281–1289` forbids adding `filter`, `transform`, `backdrop-filter`, `contain` or `will-change` to it precisely so that portaled sheets and viewport-positioned popups keep working. A child of the frame can therefore paint above the masthead or below the frame's bottom edge; nothing about the frame prevents it. What confines a sheet today is the clip's own geometry ([F03], [F04]) and the frame's z-index ([F02]). **(verified)**

**[F06] The canvas clamp already knows the geometry a "fully visible" panel needs.** The top-anchor path (`tug-sheet.tsx:1055–1253`) reads the canvas element (`paneFrameEl.parentElement`), takes the tighter of the canvas bottom and `window.innerHeight`, and writes an inline `max-height` so the panel ends `SHEET_CANVAS_GAP` (32px, `:232`) above the visible bottom, observing the canvas, the frame and the window. It is the same rectangle math panes use when drag-resizing. Nothing in it is specific to the top anchor except the clip it writes into. **(verified)**

**[F07] The pane raise is a React-assigned inline style, but the frame also reads DOM attributes for form.** The z-index lands as `style={{ zIndex }}` on the frame (`tug-pane.tsx:4576`), computed by the deck from store order ([F02]). The fold is worn as `data-minimized` on the same element, projected from the pane record and consumed by CSS only (`tug-pane.tsx` prop comment near `:2088`; `tug-pane.css:309`). A sheet-open state written the same way — an attribute on the frame, read by a CSS rule — would be appearance through DOM, which is [L06]'s door. **(verified)**

**[F08] The Z2 telemetry placards are already canvas-tier and are not clipped by any of this.** They are `TugPopover` surfaces, which render through Radix `Popover.Portal` into the canvas overlay root (`tug-popover.tsx:785`), a `position: fixed; inset: 0` layer at `--tug-z-overlay-popup` (9200) above every pane (`canvas-overlay-root.tsx`, `styles/chrome.css:38–44`). Their list frame is capped at Radix's collision-computed available height so "a long list can NEVER push the popup off-screen" (`tug-popup-list.css:57–64`). Their collision boundary defaults to the viewport (`tug-popover.tsx:684`), and no call site narrows it. On a folded card the Z2 row is not `inert` — only the entry region and the view slot are ([F04]'s CSS and the fold effect in `session-card.tsx`) — so the cells stay clickable. **(verified in code; not reproduced on a folded card. If a placard is ever seen clipped there, it is a separate defect and not this brief's.)**

**[F09] The pane-modal doctrine draws its line at modality, not at paint.** `pane-model.md:465–482` gives the reason for the frame portal: choosing the canvas tier "for a surface that is supposed to be modal-to-one-Pane sets up an entire class of bleed bugs." The bleed it names is *interaction* bleed — a sheet in one pane blocking another — and every mechanism that prevents it is already pane-scoped and independent of where the panel paints: the scrim is `.tug-pane-scrim` inside the chrome (`tug-pane.css:1331`), modality is `inert` on this pane's `.tug-pane-body` (`tug-sheet.tsx:1587–1600`), and the focus scope is `loop` without `trapped` (`:1816–1830`). A panel painting over a peer's pixels does not touch any of them. **(verified)**

**[F10] The overlay tier already reserves a slot for a canvas-tier sheet that no longer exists.** `styles/chrome.css:25` lists `--tug-z-overlay-dialog` (9400) as "TugSheet (canvas-tier sheet)", and `tug-sheet.tsx:1581–1585` records that the canvas-tier wrapper and its anchor applier were retired in favour of the frame portal, because with the panel in the frame "when the pane moves or resizes, the panel follows via the frame's own layout." The token survives only as the base `TugSheetStackingContext` measures popup-in-dialog tiers from. **(verified)**

## Decisions {#decisions}

**[B01] Sheets stay portaled into the pane frame; what changes is the clip's geometry and the frame's paint order, not the portal target.** [F05] shows the frame does not clip and [F09] shows modality does not depend on paint. So the two defects are reachable without moving the panel: the clip is allowed to extend past the frame in *both* directions ([B02]) and the frame is lifted above its peers while a sheet is up ([B03]). This keeps the property the retired canvas tier gave up ([F10]) — the panel follows the pane through drags, resizes and column reflows with no applier and no observer of its own — and it keeps `pane-model.md`'s table true: the portal target, scope and hook are unchanged. Revisit only if a case appears that the frame's coordinate space cannot express, which the geometry in [B02] argues does not exist.

**[B02] A sheet is sized and placed against the visible canvas, prefers its rest line, and grows past the frame — down first, then up — before it scrolls.** The bottom-anchor effect's `floor` ([F03]) moves from the frame's bottom to the canvas bottom the top-anchor clamp already measures ([F06]), `SHEET_CANVAS_GAP` above it and never below the viewport. When the panel still does not fit below the masthead, the clip's top is allowed to go negative — above the masthead, up to the canvas top plus the same gap — and only a panel taller than the canvas itself scrolls, which is the limit the window imposes and not one the pane does. The rest line stays the preference for a panel that fits, so a sheet on an ordinary card is placed exactly where `modal-rest-line.md` puts it today and nothing about the common case moves. What this rules out is any per-pane cap on a sheet's height; what it keeps is the doctrine that the region below the rest line "is exactly what a modal is entitled to paint over," now extended to the canvas the pane stands on.

**[B03] While a sheet is open, its pane paints above every peer pane, regardless of focus.** The frame gets an attribute written by the sheet on open and cleared on close, and one CSS rule lifts a frame carrying it above `CARD_ZINDEX_BASE + N` for any N — a flat value below the sidebar band at `SIDEBAR_PANE_ZINDEX_BASE` (8990) and the overlay root at 9000, so rails and popovers keep their order over it. This is appearance through a DOM attribute and a stylesheet rule ([F07], [L06]); the deck's focus-order z-index map is not consulted and not changed, so focusing another pane still reorders the rest of the wall as it does today. The raise applies whether the sheet was clicked open or raised by a watcher, because the second screenshot is the watcher case and it is the one focus order cannot answer ([F02]). What this trades away is a peer pane's right to paint over a neighbour's open sheet; a sheet is a surface the user is being asked to read or answer, and there is no case where the reader wants it partly under something else. Because the inline `zIndex` on the frame outranks a stylesheet rule, the implementation either publishes the deck's value as a custom property the stylesheet reads back or applies the lift with `!important`; the brief decides the mechanism is CSS keyed on an attribute and leaves the spelling to the arc.

**[B04] A rest line with no box is treated as no rest line.** `modal-rest-line.md` already rules that "a host without a view slot matches nothing and falls back to the sheet's default top anchor." A folded card's slot exists but is `display: none` ([F04]), and the anchor effect must not run its arithmetic on an all-zero rect. The effect checks the anchor's box (a zero-height rect, or `offsetParent === null`) and stands down to the top anchor when it finds none, which puts a folded card's sheets where its `Compacting` cover already goes: dropped from the masthead, over whatever is below, sized by [B02]. This is the existing fallback extended by one clause, not a new rule; it is also what makes the fold and the unfold safe mid-sheet, since a slot that regains its box is re-measured by the same `ResizeObserver` the effect already holds on the anchor.

**[B05] `presentation: "rise"` follows the anchor, not the call site.** A sheet that has fallen back to the top anchor under [B04] should not rise from a line that is not there. The entrance is derived from the resolved anchor at open time, so a folded card's sheets drop in the way its cover does, and the same sheet on the same card rises again once the card is unfolded. Call sites keep passing the pair `modal-rest-line.md` prescribes; the sheet decides what the pair means when the line is absent.

**[B06] `pane-model.md` is amended to say what [F09] found: modality is pane-scoped, paint is not.** The sentence "peer Panes z-stacked above paint above the modal panel automatically" is replaced by the rule in [B03], and the reasoning paragraph keeps its point — interaction bleed is prevented structurally by scrim, `inert` and focus scope — without claiming that paint containment is part of the mechanism. `modal-rest-line.md` gains [B02]'s growth order and [B04]'s boxless clause. The clip's CSS comment at `tug-sheet.css:63–68` is rewritten to match, since it is the line a future reader will cite back at this brief.

## Open Questions {#open-questions}

- **Should a folded card raise the `Compacting` cover at all?** The fold is "a run of sessions being watched rather than talked to," and the masthead beat already reads `Compacting context…` (second screenshot). A cover that holds the card is doing work the beat already does, and under [B03] it will now paint over the card the user is typing in. The alternative is to suppress the cover on a folded card and let the beat carry it, raising the cover only if the card is unfolded mid-run. This changes what gets built — a fold-aware guard in `session-compaction-run.tsx` rather than nothing — and it is the user's call about what a folded card is for, not a fact the code can settle.

## Non-goals {#non-goals}

- **Portaling sheets to the canvas overlay root.** The user's own suggestion, and the obvious one. Rejected because it reintroduces the anchor applier and observers `[F10]` retired — a canvas-tier panel has to be told every time its pane moves, resizes, or is reflowed by the imposer — and because [F05] and [F09] show the frame already permits everything the overlay would give: paint outside the frame, and order above peers. Recorded here so that "just portal it" is not re-proposed the next time a sheet is seen behind a pane; if [B02]/[B03] ever prove insufficient, this is the fallback and the reason it was not taken first.
- **Touching the Z2 telemetry placards.** They are canvas-tier already ([F08]). Nothing here changes `TugPopover`, `TugPopupList` or the overlay root.
- **Raising the pane through the deck's focus order.** Considered: dispatch a focus/raise on sheet open so the existing z-index map answers. Rejected: it steals focus from the pane the user is working in whenever a watcher raises a cover, and it routes a paint-only concern through store state that [L06] says appearance must not use.
- **A per-form sheet layout for folded cards** — a compact panel, a different sheet, a placard instead. The folded card is a first-class form and its sheets are the same sheets; [B02] and [B04] make the ordinary panel fit by giving it the canvas, which is what a form-specific layout would also have had to do.
- **Changing what folds.** The view slot's `display: none` at rest is a deliberate cost decision (`session-minimize-reveal-brief.md` [B06]) and is not reopened; [B04] works around the missing box rather than restoring it.
- **The scrim.** It stays inside the chrome and stays pane-sized. A panel that paints past the frame under [B02] paints over unscrimmed canvas and peers, which is what the top-anchored clip has done since it was written.

## Exit {#exit}

An arc, in one landing, with a measurement in front of it.

The first move is the DevTools read named in [F03]: open the AI settings sheet on a card whose composer holds a long prompt, and compare the panel's computed height with the clip's. It decides whether the top-cut in the first screenshot is the floor alone or the floor plus an inner column refusing the cap — [B02] is needed either way, but the second answer adds a fix to `.tug-sheet-content`'s inner layout that the first does not.

The body is in `tug-sheet.tsx` and `tug-sheet.css`: the bottom-anchor effect learns the canvas floor and the upward growth ([B02]), stands down on a boxless anchor ([B04]), derives the entrance from the resolved anchor ([B05]), and writes the sheet-open attribute on the frame that the lift rule reads ([B03]). The doctrine edits ([B06]) land with it.

Verification is on the running app in three forms, each with the AI settings sheet and with a `Compacting` cover: an unfolded card in a wall with a card focused below it, a folded card in the same position, and a card in a short pane whose composer is tall. In every one the panel's full extent is on screen, its top is under nothing, its bottom is above the canvas edge, and the card below is painted under it. Then the ordinary case: a full-height card, where the sheet must still rest on its line exactly as `at0226-usage-sheet` and `at0097-rewind-sheet` pin it today. `at0057-popup-in-sheet-stacking` and `at0100-sheet-pane-modal-focus` guard the modality [B06] promises is unchanged. The fold tests `at0550–at0557` are unaffected unless the open question above is answered yes, in which case a fold-aware cover earns a case of its own.
