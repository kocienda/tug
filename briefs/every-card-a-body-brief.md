# Every card a body: the other card kinds, the shell's diet, lifecycle and the cap, the harness sweep, and the law

**Purpose:** After stages 2–4 a Session card lives in a body, placed, moved, focused and edged by the host and the shell, behind a switch the user has had on since 2026-10-14. Every other card kind still mounts in the shell's document, so two code paths run for one thing, the shell still carries the Overview's whole population in its own document, the harness addresses one view by default, and no law names the shape. This brief moves every card kind into a body, removes the in-document card path and what only it needed, rules lifecycle and the ceiling on live bodies, migrates the tests that reach across documents, and writes the law.

Stage 5 of `briefs/per-card-web-views-program-brief.md`. Amended 2026-10-15 against stages 2a (`afc3dd3a1`), 3 (`4cb4b8974`) and 4 (`7eae2b44e`).

## Purpose {#purpose}

The user: "one WKWebView per card." This stage is where that becomes true for every card and where the single-document deck stops existing. The asks it serves are the ones the program opened on (`briefs/graphics-animations-asks.md`): the flow slide that "often drops many frames", the workspace switch that must never pause, and the Overview, "the largest thing on the deck, not skipped when unseen, and growing as sessions work", which still sits in the shell's document and pays into every frame's compositing walk.

## Decisions {#decisions}

**[B01] Every card kind becomes a body, and the gates become a registry fact.** The body's boot registers every kind the shell registers, held equal by the same kind of enumeration test as `body-stores.test.ts`; the hand-off's `componentId` check becomes a per-kind `body: true` on the registration, so a kind is moved by flipping its registration and nothing else, and a kind not yet moved still mounts in the document. The order: the Overview first, because it is the largest, grows with the day and is already windowed; then Cards, Jots, Arcs and Layout; then text, file-view, diff, commit; then devtools, the singletons and hello; then gallery, spike and fixtures, which the harness mounts. A rail is a pane like any other and its card a body like any other. The one spare serves every kind ([F05]).

**[B02] A kind moves only when it passes the grounding bar.** Each kind's move lands with the rows of `tests/app-test/body-grounding/` that apply to it, run against that kind: arrival (row 1), drag (2), departure (3), slide and the host's beats (4, 14, 17), the keyboard it owns (5, 7, 23), the menus (6), its sheets and surfaces (8, 22, 25, 26), fold (9), workspace (10), lifecycle (11), paste and drops (12, 27), facts (28), order (29), edges (21), and no hole (20). The README gains a column per kind; a row that does not apply to a kind says so there. The reading on the real binary is per kind as grounding [B05] rules. A kind that needs a new row (the Overview's composer inserting into a Session body; the Cards card's deck reads) adds it.

**[B03] What a sidebar card read from the deck, it reads from the shell.** The Overview and Cards cards keep their all-sessions stores, which a body already attaches ([F02]); the Overview's prompt insert becomes `card-command {insert-prompt}` over the bus to the target Session body, with the shell routing by card id; the Cards card reads the arrangement from a `deck-facts` projection the shell already sends for menus (`DeckFacts.menu`), widened to what the Cards card lists; the Layout card's rows stay deck actions forwarded as they are. Nothing in a sidebar card's component learns it is in a body; the bridge satisfies the contexts, as it does for the Session card.

**[B04] The shell's diet.** Once every kind has a body and the switch is on: `CardHost` renders only placeholders; `CardPortal`, `pane-content-registry`, `selection-guard.ts`, `registerCrossingInterior` and the crossing CustomEvents, `resize-episode`'s in-document dispatch, the ingest hold on the shell's connection, the Overview window's document reads, and the in-document arrival quiet all leave; the shell's `CanvasOverlayRoot` keeps the shell's own surfaces and the shell-drawn ones. The shell subscribes to no session feed; its mastheads read `card-facts`. The shell's census on the user's deck is read before and after and goes in the record; the target is the chrome alone, a few thousand elements that do not grow with the day. This step runs only with the switch on; with it off the shell is the stage 6 deck until [B08].

**[B05] Lifecycle per body stands as built, with the shell's termination added.** Grounding's row 11 is the bar ([F03]). What is not yet built: when the shell's WebContent terminates, the host reloads the shell and re-places every body from the shell's next placement, bodies kept alive through it; a body's termination tells the shell so the pane shows its restoring state while the host reloads it. A tugcast restart re-authenticates the shell, whose cookie every body shares, and every page reconnects. The row reads both terminations through the harness's kill, on the real binary.

**[B06] The cap: hidden bodies unload past a budget.** A hidden body (a parked workspace's card, a folded card past a count, a card scrolled out of a stack) is the first to be unloaded: the host destroys the view, the body's state bag and session seating stay in tugbank and tugcast, and the body is re-created on reveal behind the arrival's reveal gate, under the lid, as grounding's row 1 reads an arrival. The budget is a count of live bodies and a resident-memory ceiling, set in `.tugtool/config.toml` and shown in the Layout card beside the Bodies row, with a default the reading justifies from [F04]: enough for the user's twenty cards at the creation cost, and a ceiling that unloads before the streaming cost of them all would. A session keeps running in tugcast whether or not its body is loaded. The reveal of an unloaded body is read against row 1's bar: no frame of canvas, cold time in the record.

**[B07] The harness sweep.** `App.body(cardId)` is the only way a test reaches a card's DOM; `cardHost()` and the shared selectors become body-scoped; the files that reach into a card's DOM from the shell's evaluator ([F06]) are migrated by a codemod that rewrites a `document.querySelector` whose selector enters a card's DOM into the scoped form, with whatever the codemod cannot prove rewritten by hand; `@covers` keeps selection scoped as each file lands, and a file lands with the kind whose card it reaches. A file that cannot be migrated is deleted with its reason in the commit, not carried red. The core tier runs once after the harness changes, as the advisory says. No shim: a test that reads a body through the shell's document is a test that does not run.

**[B08] The instruments address every page, and then the switch goes.** The gap sampler and `__tugMotion` run in every page; `tugtool deck motion` prints them per page beside the window's frame row, `--streaming` counts every body's composer, `gaps` reads every page's ring, `walk` prices each body's document. Then the record's final row ([B09]) is read, and only then: the Bodies row's default flips to on by a decision written into the program brief after the user has run the all-bodies deck as their daily deck and said so, the Layout row stays as the way back for one release, and the in-document path and `bodyViewsEnabled` leave in the step after, which is the last step of the arc and the one that makes [B04]'s diet unconditional.

**[B09] The law and the record.** A new `tuglaws/shell-and-bodies.md` names: one shell, one body per card, one process each; the shell as the authority on arrangement and the policy of focus, the host as the mover of bodies and the follower of first responder; the three channels and what each carries; set-up-and-go as executed in two places on one clock with the shipped offset; the lid and the hole and the order they go on and off; the cap and the unload rule; what a test may assume about documents. `tuglaws.md` gains the law lines; `design-decisions.md` the reversals: the one-document deck, cross-card selection, the in-page settle moving cards, the ingest hold as a deck-wide hold. The headroom program's escape clause is closed as taken. The program record's final row: the four gestures on the user's aged deck with three streams, processes and resident memory with twenty cards open, the shell's census, and the window's frame reading, beside the before.

## Open Questions {#open-questions}

- **Does the Layout card stay a body?** Its rows drive the shell's imposition and it is small; a body for consistency, by [B01], unless its rows' round trips through a resize read as a cost. Decided in the arc by the reading.
- **What is the default cap?** [F04]'s numbers against the machine's memory; written beside the setting with the reading that set it.
- **Does the Overview's size still cost the shell anything once it is a body?** The compositing walk is priced by the shell's document; the Overview leaving it is the point. Read by the census before and after ([B04]) and by the flow slide's rows with the Overview open in a rail, as stage 3's row 16 read it.

## Non-goals {#non-goals}

- **Keeping an in-document path for any card.** One shape, as the user asked; a card that cannot be a body is a defect to fix, not an exception.
- **Compatibility shims in the harness.** Migrated, not bridged.
- **Changing what any card shows.** Only where it runs.
- **Flipping the switch's default before the user's daily run.** [B08]'s order; grounding [B07] stands.
- **Re-reading stage 3's motion bar per kind.** The host moves a container; what is in it does not change the beats. Row 14 runs once per kind as a check, not as a new reading.
