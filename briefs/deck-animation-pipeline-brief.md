# The deck's animation pipeline: compositor-only, pre-armed, measured

**Purpose:** Activating a card in another slot of the current workspace sometimes slides and sometimes lands as a cut, on an idle app with no live sessions. The cause is not intent and not a busy main thread; it is raster work the compositor is handed at the two instants the eye judges a slide, and it grows with the number of session cards on the deck. This brief pins where that work comes from and decides the shape of a pipeline in which it cannot arise.

---

## Purpose {#purpose}

The user, on the gesture as it stands:

> Cards flow in 4 slots. When I click in the Workspaces sidebar card to go between the card in slot 1 and the card in slot 4, sometimes I get a slide and sometimes I don't. I imagine that this has something to do with dropped frames, rather than an intent to crossfade. So, my question is: *WHY ARE WE DROPPING FRAMES?* There are no active sessions. The app is otherwise doing no work. This *must* work smoothly *even when we are running several sessions at once*.

And on what is wanted:

> I want a *comprehensively correct* animation pipeline. I don't want half measures. I don't want to spend time messing around with hacks.

Two constraints, then. The slide must be smooth on an idle deck of four session cards, and it must stay smooth as the session-card count rises — so the answer has to be one whose per-frame cost does not scale with how many cards are on screen or how large they are, rather than one that happens to fit on today's deck.

## Evidence {#evidence}

**[F01] The gesture is one three-slot reveal through `raiseCard` plus a border flash, and no test samples its motion.** A Cards-card row click dispatches `focus-session-card` (`tugdeck/src/components/cards/cards-card.tsx:1570`), handled at `tugdeck/src/action-dispatch.ts:964` as `raiseCard(...)` then `flashPaneBorder(pane.id)`. `raiseCard` (`tugdeck/src/focus-transfer.ts:1142`) runs `transferFocusForActivation` (`:780`): save and deactivation callbacks on the outgoing card, `flushSync(activateCard)`, `applyBagFocus`, `blurFocusInOutgoingCard`. The one existing slide pin, `tests/app-test/at0621-intra-workspace-slide.test.ts:383`, drives `go-to-slot` and then three `focus-card right` hops 140ms apart — three one-slot slides, no flash, no Cards-card path — and `at0454-flow-mode` dispatches `focus-session-card` but asserts only keyboard-ring state. **(verified, read from the code)**

**[F02] The commit itself is sound: one notify, transform-only keyframes, sensible count.** `activateCard` (`tugdeck/src/deck-manager.ts:1808`, `:4380`) is one `_flipFirstResponder` commit carrying the reveal terms, so `flowOffset` moves three slots in one write of `--tug-imposer-flow-offset` (`tugdeck/src/components/chrome/deck-canvas.tsx:2884`), which every pane's `left: calc(...)` reads (`tugdeck/src/lib/layout-imposer.ts:1631`). The settle's move beat animates `transform` only for a pure flow slide (`tugdeck/src/lib/pane-flip.ts`, `springSettleKeyframes`), sampled at 60Hz for ~24 keyframes over the 400ms nominal (`tugdeck/src/components/tugways/physics.ts:22–25`, `IMPOSITION_SETTLE_MS`), launched through `el.animate()` (`tugdeck/src/components/tugways/tug-animator.ts:276`) with `fill: "none"`. Nothing here is the defect. **(verified)**

**[F03] Pane frames are not compositor layers at rest, and that is a stated design decision.** `tugdeck/src/components/tugways/tug-pane.css:1473–1480`: "NOTHING lands on `.tug-pane` itself — no `filter`, no `transform`, no `backdrop-filter`, no `contain`, no `will-change`. Any of those would make every receded frame a containing block for its `position: fixed` descendants, and `TugSheet` portals into the frame while completion popups, alerts, and banners position from viewport coordinates." So when four `transform` animations start, the engine must promote four frames to layers and rasterize each whole session card — masthead, composer, transcript — at Retina scale inside the first frame; when the tweens end (`fill: "none"` → `commitStyles` → `cancel`), the layers are dropped and the content is rasterized again into the parent. Two raster spikes, at the start and at the landing, each proportional to session-card count and size. **(verified as a structural fact from the code; the spike durations are not yet measured — [B09] measures them)**

**[F04] The focus flip transitions two blended full-pane overlays for exactly the length of the tween.** Every unfocused pane wears a `mix-blend-mode: saturation` layer (`tug-pane.css:1428`), a `mix-blend-mode: multiply` wash (`:1450`, brio) and a dim `::after` on the frame (`tugdeck/styles/chrome.css:230`). On activation the outgoing pane gains them and the incoming one loses them, and both `transition` over `--tugx-imposer-settle-duration` (`tug-pane.css:1446`, `:1459`) — the same 400ms the move beat runs. A blended layer whose opacity or colour changes every frame is re-blended and re-rasterized every frame, so during the slide two of the four panes repaint at full size on every frame while all four translate. **(verified, read from the code)**

**[F05] The flash is a paint-property animation on a full-pane pseudo, 1750ms, started in the same frame as the slide.** `@keyframes tug-pane-border-flash` animates `box-shadow` on `.tug-pane-flash::before` covering the frame (`tug-pane.css:777–795`). `box-shadow` is not a compositor property; the pseudo repaints per frame for nearly two seconds. `flashPaneBorder` also forces a synchronous reflow (`void paneEl.offsetWidth`, `tugdeck/src/lib/flash-pane-border.ts`) on a tree React has just dirtied, inside the click task. **(verified)**

**[F06] The click task does layout reads proportional to pane count before the first frame.** `arm` runs synchronously inside the notify: per shown frame a `getBoundingClientRect`, then `beginResizeEpisode` → `discoverScrollers` → `watchGeneric` → `firstBoxReaching`, which walks children with `getComputedStyle` and `getBoundingClientRect` up to twelve deep (`tugdeck/src/lib/resize-episode.ts:404`, `:316`). The Last pass measures every frame again. Then `applyBagFocus` reaches a bare `target.focus()` (`tugdeck/src/default-focus.ts:206`; `preventScroll` is an opt-in this path does not take) on the incoming CodeMirror editor. None of this drops a frame *during* the tween — a long task delays the animation's start rather than skipping it, because a WAAPI animation is pending until its first rendering opportunity — but it is the budget the first frame is already spending when [F03]'s raster arrives. **(verified, read from the code)**

**[F07] Transcript cells opt into `content-visibility: auto`, which the engine resolves against the viewport during motion.** `tugdeck/src/components/tugways/tug-list-view.css:349`. A pane translating three slots carries cells across the relevance boundary mid-tween, and each newly relevant cell is styled, laid out and painted on demand inside the animation. The file's own comment records that `content-visibility` outranks compositing. **(inferred from the code; not measured — [B09]'s sampler measures it)**

**[F08] Why it is intermittent.** The move beat's spring is critically damped (`crossing: { zeta: 1.0 }`, `tugdeck/src/lib/imposer-motion.ts:87`), so most of the travel is in the first third of the 400ms. A first-frame raster hole of roughly 80–120ms therefore swallows most of the visible motion: the picture arrives already mostly moved, and the eye reads a cut. Whether the hole fits in one frame's budget varies with GPU state, whether tiles are resident, and whether the transcript cells had been painted, which is why the same gesture sometimes slides and sometimes does not. **(inference from [F03]–[F07]; the sampler of [B09] is what confirms the hole and its size)**

**[F09] What was ruled out.** `isDevEnv()` gating `validateDeckState` on every notify reads as true in a browser at source, but the Release bundle compiles it to `return !1`. The `workspace-switch-quiet` arc's switch epoch is not joined, not in the Release build, and inert for intra-workspace moves. The session-notification hold (`holdNotifications`) is moot with no live session. Keyframe count and property choice are right ([F02]). **(verified)**

**[F10] The dependency that forbids compositing is real but narrow.** The descendants that position from the viewport are in `tug-alert.css`, `tug-banner.css`, `tug-completion-menu.css`, `tug-modal-input-dialog.css`, `tug-pane-bulletin.css`, `tug-key-sink.css`, `tug-markdown-view.css` and `tug-pane.css` itself. `TugSheet` portals into the frame *deliberately* so the panel follows the pane through every move (`tugdeck/src/components/tugways/tug-sheet.tsx:12`, [D19], [D20]) — which is compatible with a transformed frame, since an absolutely positioned descendant follows a transformed ancestor; only `position: fixed` descendants break. **(verified, read from the code)**

## Decisions {#decisions}

**[B01] Every pane frame is a compositor layer at rest, permanently.** The frame carries a promoting declaration from the moment it mounts — a standing `will-change: transform` (or an identity `transform`, whichever the measurement of [B09] shows the engine treats as a stable layer) — so no gesture ever creates or destroys a layer. This is the whole of the answer to [F03] and it is the one decision the rest hang from: a slide over pre-existing layers is a translate the compositor performs alone, and its cost is independent of how many session cards are on the deck or how tall their transcripts are. Toggling promotion per gesture is rejected as the hack it is ([Non-goals]): promoting at frame 1 *is* the spike. The memory cost of standing layers is an open question this brief measures rather than guesses at.

**[B02] No `position: fixed` element lives inside a pane frame; viewport-positioned surfaces move to a deck-level overlay host.** [B01] makes every frame a containing block for fixed descendants, and [F10] names exactly which surfaces that breaks: completion menus, alerts, banners, bulletins, the modal input dialog, the key sink, the markdown view's fixed pieces. Each is re-homed to a single overlay layer that is a child of the deck root rather than of any frame, positioned from measured anchor rects (the pattern the gauges already use for cross-subtree geometry, `tugdeck/src/lib/imposer-gauges.ts`), and lifted in z the way a pane holding an open sheet already is. `TugSheet` stays in the frame — following the pane is its stated contract and an absolutely positioned descendant follows a transformed ancestor correctly — and any of its pieces that are `fixed` today become `absolute`. The rule that forbade compositing on `.tug-pane` is retired and replaced by this one, which is enforceable: a stylesheet guard that fails on `position: fixed` under a pane-scoped selector.

**[B03] While any frame is in a settle, nothing on the deck changes a property the compositor cannot animate alone.** `transform` and `opacity`, on layers that exist at rest — that is the complete list. This is written as a law beside the existing motion laws and enforced two ways: a stylesheet guard that fails on a `transition` or `animation` naming any other property on a selector that can match a frame or its overlays, and a runtime assertion in the settle's own trace that no such animation is running on a frame during the beat. It is what turns [F04] and [F05] from things that happened to be true into things that cannot recur.

**[B04] The recede overlays exist at rest on every frame and change only by opacity, sequenced after the move lands.** Both blend layers ([F04]) are present on focused and unfocused panes alike — at opacity 0 on the focused one — so the flip creates and destroys no layer, and their only animated property is `opacity`, which the compositor can run on a blended layer without re-rasterizing its content (this is the one claim in this brief the sampler must confirm on a real deck before it is relied on, see Open Questions). The fade is not concurrent with the move: it runs as the settle's last act, on the settle's own completion, so the frames are already still when the recede paints. If the measurement shows blended layers cannot be made compositor-only at all, the fallback is a precomposed desaturated snapshot swapped by opacity — a different implementation of the same rule, not a relaxation of it.

**[B05] The flash is a compositor-only ring that starts when the settle lands.** A pre-drawn ring element with a static `box-shadow`, animated on `opacity` alone, standing on its own layer; the `void offsetWidth` reflow is gone with the class-restart idiom it existed for. It begins on the settle's completion rather than in the click's frame, so the two seconds it runs overlap no motion. The flash is a product feature and is rebuilt, not removed ([Non-goals]).

**[B06] The click task is bounded and does no forced layout of its own.** The action handler makes no synchronous layout read after React has committed; `arm`'s per-frame reads are coalesced to one measurement pass per frame per pane and never walk a subtree during the commit that starts a settle — the anchor discovery of [F06] moves to the moment an episode actually needs to re-anchor, which is a resize, not a translate; the activation focus write passes `preventScroll` on the deck's own path. None of these is the cause of the cut, and none is a half measure: they are what keeps the first frame's budget for the compositor.

**[B07] A pane in a settle holds its cells' relevance.** While a frame carries the settling mark, its list views do not let `content-visibility: auto` re-resolve which cells are relevant — the cells rendered when the settle armed stay rendered until it lands. Whether this rule is load-bearing on today's deck is [F07]'s open measurement; the rule stands either way because it is the same principle as [B03] applied to layout: the frame's content is decided before it moves and does not change while it moves.

**[B08] Every rule above has a sampler, and the sampler drives the user's gesture.** A new app-test seeds four session cards in a four-up flow deck and activates slot 4 from slot 1 through `focus-session-card` — the real path, including the flash — recording per `requestAnimationFrame` the timestamp, the move animation's `currentTime`, and every shown frame's rect. It asserts: no inter-frame gap longer than two frames at the display's rate across the tween and the flip; the animation's first painted frame is within one frame of its start time; no frame's computed opacity is below 1; nothing changes a rect after the beat lands. It runs the same assertions with eight session cards, which is the scaling constraint stated in the purpose. It must run with the harness window uncovered — a covered window suspends rAF, which is the occlusion condition already recorded — and it notes the rAF tick count so a suspended run reads as suspended rather than as smooth.

**[B09] The recording comes first, and it attributes before anything is rebuilt.** Before a byte of [B01]–[B07] lands, the sampler of [B08] is run against the deck as it is, and then under `tugtool file probe` with each suspect removed in turn: a temporary standing promotion on `.tug-pane`, the overlay transitions set to `none`, the flash disabled, offscreen-skip disabled. The readings name which of [F03]–[F07] carries the hole and how large each is. The design above does not change with the answer — a pipeline in which none of them can happen is the goal regardless — but the order of the work and the measurement each rule is held to do. The 2026-09-18 drop fix was "verified" green without a sampler and was not fixed; this work does not repeat that.

**[B10] The settle records its own frame gaps.** While a settle is running — and only then, on the same rAF pump that ends with it — the canvas records the longest inter-frame gap and the count of gaps over one frame into a `settle-frames` trace event beside `settle-arm` and `settle-release`. This is the product measuring itself so a regression is a number in the trace rather than a feeling at the keyboard, and it costs nothing when no settle is running.

## Open Questions {#open-questions}

- **What four and eight standing session-card layers cost in GPU memory, and whether the engine keeps them stable.** A standing `will-change: transform` on a Retina-scale pane holds a texture per pane. The sampler of [B08] at eight cards, read alongside the WebKit/Chromium layer tree, answers whether the cost is acceptable and whether the layer survives at rest; if a permanent promotion proves too costly, the alternative is a promotion held from the first pointer-down or key-down on the deck to the settle's landing — still never at frame 1 of a tween — and the brief's rule is unchanged.

- **Whether a `mix-blend-mode` layer's opacity can be composited without re-rasterizing its content.** [B04] rests on it. The blend is against the pane's own content within `.tug-pane-chrome`'s isolation, so in principle the blended result can be a cached layer whose opacity is a compositor property — but this has to be seen on the real deck, not assumed. If it cannot, [B04]'s stated fallback applies.

- **Which fixed-positioned surfaces of [F10] have positioning logic that assumes the frame is not a containing block.** Reading each stylesheet answers the CSS half; the TypeScript that computes anchor rects for the completion menu and the popovers has to be read too, since a surface that measures its anchor with `getBoundingClientRect` is already viewport-relative and moves cleanly, while one that reads `offsetTop` against the frame does not.

## Non-goals {#non-goals}

- **Toggling `will-change` around each gesture.** It is the obvious small change and it is the hack this brief exists to refuse: promotion at the start of the tween is exactly the raster spike of [F03], and demotion at the end is the second one. A layer that exists only while it is needed is created at the moment it is needed most.

- **Longer or slower tweens, or a delayed start to hide the spike.** They trade a cut for a pause and leave the cost in place; the cost is what has to go.

- **Removing the recede or the flash.** Both are product decisions about how a deck reads. They are rebuilt as compositor-only motion ([B04], [B05]), not taken away.

- **A `contain`/`content-visibility` treatment of the frame itself as the promotion mechanism.** `contain: paint` or `content-visibility` on `.tug-pane` would also make it a containing block and would clip the sheet's slide-in and every popover; [B01] promotes with `transform`/`will-change` and [B02] moves what that breaks.

- **Hand-tuned timing offsets between the move, the recede and the flash.** [B04] and [B05] sequence on the settle's completion, which is a fact about the frame, not on a constant that has to be re-guessed when the tween changes.

- **Fixing the switch-epoch or workspace-dissolve work again.** That is the `workspace-switch-quiet` arc's and is inert here.

## Exit {#exit}

An arc. Its first step is the sampler of [B08] run as the recording of [B09] — the user's gesture, four and eight session cards, each suspect probed out in turn — so the work starts from a measured attribution rather than from this brief's inference. After that, in the order the dependencies run: the overlay host and the re-homing of every fixed-positioned surface ([B02]), because nothing can be promoted until nothing inside a frame breaks under promotion; then standing promotion of the frames ([B01]) with the memory reading of the first open question taken at eight cards; then the compositor-only recede and flash, sequenced after the settle ([B04], [B05]) with the blend-layer question settled on the real deck; the bounded click task ([B06]) and held relevance ([B07]) beside them; the law and its two guards ([B03]) and the settle's own frame record ([B10]) landing with the rules they enforce; and last the sampler rerun as the proof, green at four and at eight, with the probe readings from the first step beside the final ones in a findings paper so before and after sit together.
