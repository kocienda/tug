### The catch-up meeting the transport

**[F01] A send queued during the catch-up is flushed onto a dead wire.** `endCatchUpWithItsBracket` (`tugdeck/src/lib/code-session-store/reducer.ts:1350`) wraps every reduction and, when an open catch-up ends, calls `sendWhatWaitedOnTheCatchUp` (`:1374`), which checks `catchUp.stage`, `phase` (`idle` or `errored`), the queue and `headIsHeld` — and never `transportState`. `handleTransportClose` on `replaying` goes `errored` and leaves `queuedSends` alone on purpose ("the queue belongs to the user"); the wrapper then flushes the head. `connection.ts:677` drops a frame on a non-open socket, so the prompt is never sent, and the reconnect's `request_inflight` finds no turn and `endTurnAsTransportLost` records it as a failed turn. Probe: `catchUp → send → replay_started → transport_close` ⇒ `phase: submitting, transport: offline, queued: 0, effects: [cancel_timer, send-frame]`. Every other flush site hangs off a turn boundary or `replay_complete`, where the wire is live; this is the one reachable from a transport loss. The rebind path (`:6728-6745`) already handles stranded sends. **(verified, read and probed)**

**[F02] The re-ask can be double-answered and re-base the window.** The `replay_in_flight` drop is inferred, not told: `holdForCatchUp` (`reducer.ts:1405-1424`) reads any `replay_complete` arriving while `behind` as "tugcode dropped my ask". tugcode (`replay-runner.ts:415-421`) and tugcast (`session.rs:930-991`) only log a drop; nothing reaches the deck. But tugcode's stdout and its stdin read are not ordered, so a close that arrives after the reveal does not prove the ask was dropped. Probe (shadow-at-sleep): window `{2, 8, hasOlder}` → inflight shadow → `noteDormant` → `catchUp()` → the shadow's `replay_complete` → two asks sent → bracket A enters as the catch-up's (window kept) → bracket B lands on an idle card with `stage none, lapsed false` as an ordinary bracket → `replayWindow = {8, 9, true}` while the transcript still holds turns 2–8. The same shape arises when the ask is front-queued during `Spawning` and tugcode's cold-boot replay runs first. The retry bound is one (`behind` cleared), which is fine; the fault is that a second answer to a one-shot ask is not recognised. `catch-up.test.ts:463,482` cover only the drop-then-answer order. **(verified, probed)**

**[F03] A bracket later than the 5 s deadline leaves a tail-only turn standing.** `handleTickCatchUp` (`reducer.ts:7741-7752`) re-applies held frames; a held live tail (`assistant_text` + `turn_complete`) commits a tail-only turn N. The late bracket (`none → open` via `lapsed`, `:6423-6425`) carries N whole but dedupes by msg-id and appends nothing (probe: `late bracket appended []`). So `catch-up.test.ts:342`'s "comes back whole, once" holds only for a bracket under 5 s; past it the partial copy is permanent. `CATCH_UP_DEADLINE_MS = 5_000` (`:1169`) is short for a `Spawning` session, whose ask tugcast front-queues and whose reveal lids the page for 5 s before the answer comes late. **(verified, probed)**

**[F04] `lapsed` is sticky across errors.** It is cleared only by `replay_asked` or a bracket opening. Probe: lapsed ask → `resume_failed` → `session_state_errored` → bare `replay_started` ⇒ `stage open`, and its close keeps `{5, 50, hasOlder: true}` though the bracket reported `{0, 50, false}`. Reachable only if a bracket arrives without `noteReplayAsked` (a respawn cold-boot on the same store); the usual rebind makes a new store. **(verified by probe; reachability is inference)**

**[F05] A lapsed re-ask forgets `keepsWindow`.** `moveCatchUp(…, "none")` rebuilds from `NO_CATCH_UP` (`reducer.ts:1331-1339`), resetting `keepsWindow` to true. A re-ask (`keepsWindow: false`, `:7720`) that lapses and is answered late keeps the loaded window's start instead of taking the read's: probe got `{0, 11, hasOlder: false}` for a bracket reporting `{7, 11, true}`. **(verified, probed)**

**[F06] A refused `begin_catch_up` still asks and arms the timer.** `handleBeginCatchUp` (`reducer.ts:7711-7731`) schedules `catch_up`, and `catchUp()` (`code-session-store.ts:1277-1289`) sends `request_replay`, even when `moveCatchUp` refused `open → asked`. Unreachable through `body-dormancy.ts:70` (`catchingUp` blocks sleep), so dead behaviour; the wrapper does not learn the move was refused. **(verified, read)**

**[F07] Exits with no test.** `transport_close` during `open` ([F01]), wire `error`, `resume_failed`/`session_state_errored`, `replay_complete{aborted}`, dispose. Scenarios with no test: the double answer ([F02]), a late bracket after a held tail ([F03]), `lapsed` across an error ([F04]), the lapsed re-ask window ([F05]), a send queued in `asked` then abandoned by the transport. What is tested asserts guarantees — turns once, window keeps its start, cycle stands, deadline re-applies, both abandon ticks, refused move, sleep/wake deadlines, late bracket, send queued then flushed — and is sound. **(verified, read)**

### Two arcs, two covers

**[F08] Two cover systems can stand over one card, and the crossing's can be a snapshot of an unpainted page.** The eviction cover (`BodyLifecycle.swift:131`) and the fully-solved-animations crossing cover (`BodyBeatsRunner.coverForCrossing`, `:222`, inserted at `:252`) share `snapshotFrame`/`AnswerOrDeadline` — the join's claim is true (`BodyViewController.swift:1385`, `BodyLifecycle.swift:353`, `BodyBeatsRunner.swift:226`) — but nothing coordinates them: no shared owner, no z rule, independent take-down (the eviction cover at `didPaint`, `BodyViewController.swift:676`; the crossing cover at `endCrossing`). ### The kept guard word

**[F15] The kept guard word does not survive a shell reload.** `evictedAsking` is a `Set` local to `installBodyShell` (`tugdeck/src/lib/body-shell.ts:655`); nothing persists or re-derives it. ## Decisions {#decisions}

Most important first.

**[B01] A queued send is flushed only onto a live wire.** `sendWhatWaitedOnTheCatchUp` requires `transportState === "online"`; when the catch-up ends by a transport loss the queue is left where it is, and the rebind path — which already handles stranded sends — sends it when the wire is back. A reducer test drives `catchUp → send → replay_started → transport_close` and asserts the send is still queued and no frame was emitted, then the rebind sends it once. Closes [F01].

**[B02] A one-shot ask carries an id, and its answer names it.** `request_replay` carries an `askId` the deck mints; tugcode echoes it on `replay_started` and `replay_complete` (tugcast passes it through; a cold-boot or inflight bracket carries none). The reducer enters a bracket as the catch-up's only when its id is the one asked; a bracket with another ask's id, or an unasked one arriving while the catch-up is `asked` or `behind`, is read as *that* ask's answer and never re-bases the window. The drop is then not inferred at all: a `replay_complete` for the *older* bracket's id while `behind` is what triggers the re-ask, and a bracket for the current id is the answer. Closes [F02]; a reducer test drives the double answer in both orders and the `Spawning` cold-boot order, and asserts the window keeps its start.

**[B03] A late bracket replaces a tail-only turn, and the deadline is the spawn's horizon.** When a bracket arrives after the deadline on a settled card, a turn it carries whole that the card holds only as a held tail is replaced, not deduped: the bracket's copy is the authority for every msg-id it names. `CATCH_UP_DEADLINE_MS` stays 5 s for a live session; for a session tugcast reports `Spawning` the deadline is armed from the spawn's own ready, not from the ask. Closes [F03]; a reducer test drives a held tail then a late bracket and asserts one whole turn.

**[B04] `lapsed` and `keepsWindow` are facts of the ask, not of the stage.** The catch-up's `lapsed` and `keepsWindow` live on the ask record and are cleared with it: `resume_failed`, `session_state_errored` and any phase change that ends a lapsed ask clear `lapsed`; `moveCatchUp(…, "none")` after a lapsed re-ask carries the re-ask's `keepsWindow` into the late bracket's close. A refused `begin_catch_up` (`open → asked`) neither asks nor arms a timer: `handleBeginCatchUp` returns the refusal and `catchUp()` reads it. Closes [F04], [F05], [F06]; reducer tests drive each.

**[B05] Every catch-up exit has a test.** Reducer tests for `transport_close` during `open`, wire `error`, `resume_failed`, `session_state_errored`, `replay_complete{aborted}` and dispose, each asserting the catch-up ended and what became of the queue. Closes [F07].

**[B06] One cover owner.** `BodyCovers` owns every cover a body can stand under — the eviction cover and the crossing cover alike — through one `standCover(for:kind:)` that admits at most one cover per body: a crossing asked for on a body already covered reuses the standing cover's image rather than snapshotting; a body that has not painted (`life.painted` false) is never snapshotted for a crossing, and `coverForCrossing` falls back to `keepsPage`. Take-down is one path keyed on the cover's kind: an eviction cover comes off at paint, a crossing cover at the track's end, and a body whose cover is both comes off at whichever is later. The budget counts standing covers (a cover standing over a body is still bytes; `standCover` moves it between two accounts of one budget), and `BodyLifecycle.swift`'s doc says so. Closes [F08], [F09]; a Swift unit test covers a crossing asked on a covered unpainted body, and a grounding row folds a revived-unpainted card.

**[B07] The body state's residues.** `BodyState.after` gains `(.bind, .evicting)` → abandon the eviction and bind, so a page that reloads itself mid-save is bound and the eviction is asked again by its clock ([F10]). A repeated `.warning` continues its episode; only a level change starts a new one ([F12]). `scheduleEviction` does not re-arm for a body `armEviction` would never arm — `guardsClose` kinds and in-hand bodies — and `decline` after a critical eviction of a guarded kind arms nothing ([F13]). The comment at `BodyViewController.swift:182-184` describes the enum's `fail` ([F14]). [F11]'s dropped clause is said under B05 of the second fixups brief and in D219.

**[B08] The kept guard word is the card's, not the shell's.** `evictedAsking` moves from a `Set` in `installBodyShell` to a card-scoped shell-owned store (`card.guardAsks`, a bus store per [D215]) that a shell reload re-reads from the deck's own state (persisted beside the binding, since it is a fact about the card's saved bag). It is cleared by the next page's first `closeGuardAsks` of either value *after* that page reports it has read its aside back (the Text card's `whenOpened` settles after the read-back; the store clears on that settle plus a publish, not on `true` alone), so a clean page clears it and a dirty one keeps it. A close cancelled by a detach shows the same refusal placard a timeout shows, with "the card's page went away" as its reason. Closes [F15], [F16], [F17]; row 38-guarded gains a reload leg, and a unit test covers the clean-page clear.

*Amended by the fourth audit's fixes ([F11] there):* the word is not persisted beside the binding. It lives in its own card-keyed defaults domain, `dev.tugapp.deck.guardasks` (`putGuardWord` / `readGuardWords` in `settings-api.ts`), one key per card. A word is a fact about a card's evicted page rather than about its saved bag, and is written and cleared on its own clock (at the eviction, at the next page's answer, at the close), so riding the bag's flush would have tied it to a write it has nothing to do with. Being card-keyed, the domain is in `CARD_KEYED_DOMAINS`, so the startup sweep prunes a word whose card is gone. The shell's `card.guardAsks` store outlives a page that fails as well as one that is evicted, and the eviction's write lands before the card is revived.
