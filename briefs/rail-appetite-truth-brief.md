# A place fits or flows its run by choice, and its appetite is the rows it draws

**Purpose:** The rail-vertical arc landed an allocator that fills a rail from what its members declare they want, and two things are wrong with it. The declarations are guesses copied from CSS written for other elements, so Layout declares less than a third of its content and is still clipped beside a Jots card holding five hundred pixels of empty list. And the allocator is one algorithm trying to answer two different questions — divide the run, or let the content be its own size — so the question of where surplus goes had to be deferred. The band already answers that pair of questions with a user choice, Fit or Flow. A rail's run wants the same choice, kept clearly apart from the deck's.

---

## Purpose {#purpose}

> "Joined and rebuilt. This took a ton of time, but I'm not sure what it did. It feels like this rail should be showing everything, but it still isn't. Why not? Why is Layout still clipped off the bottom when Jots has all this room?"

> "This gives us *one* allocation algo, right? What if we had a version of the vertical *fit and flow* algos for the sidebars?"

The screenshot after the join: a right rail carrying Cards, Jots and Layout, about 2560px of run. Cards shows three sessions and one file, then empty list. Jots shows eleven rows, then more empty list than rows. Layout shows its caption, its drawing, its preset strip and six of its nine control rows, and is cut off partway through the seventh. It is the picture `briefs/rail-vertical-allocation-brief.md` opened on, with different proportions.

That brief's `[B05]` said "Layout takes its ~400 and stops, and its surplus is redistributed to the members that still want more," and deferred two things: the per-card numbers (its `[Q02]`) and the question of where run past everyone's natural should go (its first open question). The user's verdict on the second: *"This seems idiotic that this was deferred. This is basically my ask."* The ask was that a rail make the best use of the space it has as content comes and goes. A card clipped beside an empty one is the ask unmet, whatever the census says.

The second quote is the user's own move past the fix: the deck already has two horizontal layouts — **Fit**, where every card fits the band, and **Flow**, where cards keep their width and the deck scrolls — chosen on the Layout card's LAYOUT row and named in its caption ("Four Up · Slim · Flow — the deck scrolls"). A rail's run has the same two honest answers, and the arc built both of them and then hid the choice behind a floors test.

Nine rows is about 490px on their own. The declaration omits every row. **(verified — rows counted in `layout-card.tsx:1338–1440`, pitch measured off the screenshot)**

**[F04] The one test that touches these numbers cannot see that they are wrong.** `tests/app-test/at0542-rail-vertical-allocation.test.ts` reads `LAYOUT_NATURAL_HEIGHT_PX` back from the settled `appetites` and asserts the allocation honours it as a ceiling. The plan said so on purpose: "the app test asserts a bound rather than a constant." No test asks whether a card at its declared natural actually has no internal scroll, which is what the previous brief's `[B02]` defined natural to *mean*. **(verified)**

**[F05] The surplus stage spreads by weight, so every card is asked to hold empty space.** `layout-imposer.ts:3469–3474`: once every member is at natural and pool remains, `heights[i] += pool · w_i / Σw`. **[F06] Greed ranks already order the members, and they are nearly all the same.** `greedRank` per registration: Overview 1, Cards 2, Arcs 2, Tripwires 2, Layout 2, Jots 3, default 9. **[F07] Comfort and natural are declared from state, and that part is right.** Every card publishes through `useCardAppetite` from a `useEffect` keyed on the two numbers, computed from row counts and constants, with no `ResizeObserver` and no layout read. The settle, the signature term and the store are as the previous brief's `[B03]` and `[B10]` asked. **[F08] Both vertical layouts already exist in the allocator; only the choice is missing.** `allocatePlaceHeights` returns a `standing` of `"shared"` — divide the run by the ladder — or `"overflow"` — each member at `max(floor, comfort · weight)`, stacked down a strip longer than the run that scrolls behind it on a per-place offset property, with the half-cut card as the affordance. The pins, the seams, the drop-zone tiles, the reveal rules, the autoscroll bands and the miniature all already fork on that standing. ## Decisions {#decisions}

### The appetites

**[B01] An appetite constant names the element it measures, and it is never the empty label's.** Each row-height constant is derived from the row actually drawn — its line count, type size and block padding — and its comment names the CSS rule those come from. The `.*-empty { min-block-size }` rule is a placeholder for a list with nothing in it and may not be cited as a row's height. This is a rule about provenance; the numbers are per card, below.

**[B02] Layout's appetite is computed from its rows, with the sidebar count as the variable.** `comfort` = title bar + caption + drawing + preset strip: the picture is the card's job, and the rows below it may scroll. `natural` = comfort + control row pitch × (3 + `sidebars.length`). The drawing's height is the miniature's `16 / 10` aspect at `--tugx-layouts-plan-mini-width` (300px → 187.5px), written beside the width in `layout-card.css` so the two knobs sit together. `sidebars.length` is already read in the same render, so this stays a pure function of state per `[F07]`. The constant retires.

**[B03] Cards' natural is a census by row kind.** The data source knows whether each row is a group header, a session row or a file row; the appetite sums the rows it will draw at each kind's own height, a collapsed group contributing its header alone as today. Jots keeps one row height, re-derived from the jot row's own rule. Arcs and Tripwires are re-measured the same way in the same pass, with the built card on screen — which is what the previous brief's `[Q02]` said and the arc did not do.

### The two layouts

**[B04] A place's run has two layouts, Fit and Flow, and the choice is the user's per place.** **Fit**: the run is the constraint. Members divide it by the ladder — floors, comfort by greed, the discretionary pool by weight capped at natural. **Flow**: the content is the constraint. Every member stands at its natural height, the strip is as long as that makes it, and it scrolls behind the run; nobody is stretched and nobody is squeezed. These are `[F08]`'s two standings made a choice, and the argument is the band's own: whether the deck should fit the screen or the screen should be a window onto the deck is not a question the deck can answer, so it does not try — and the same is true one axis over. The deferred question dissolves: in fit the run must be filled so slack goes somewhere (`[B06]`); in flow there is no slack to allocate, because flow never promised to fill the run.

**[B05] The layout is stored per place beside its mode, defaults to fit, and is additive on the blob.** `RailArrangement.layout?: PlaceLayout` and `ColumnArrangement.layout?: PlaceLayout`, with `PlaceLayout = "fit" | "flow"` — the same two words as `ImpositionLayout` and a *distinct type*, so a deck layout can never be handed where a place layout is read or the reverse. Absent reads as fit, matching the deck's default. `parseRails` / `parseColumns` read it as defensively as they read `mode`. Actions `SET_RAIL_LAYOUT { side, layout }` and `SET_COLUMN_LAYOUT { slot, layout }` beside the mode actions. Like mode, order and shares, it is never deleted by membership churn ([L23]). It is meaningful only under split; a stacked place stores it and ignores it, as it stores shares.

**[B06] In fit, run past everyone's natural goes entirely to the greediest member, never spread.** When every member is at natural and pool remains, the whole remainder goes to the lowest `greedRank`, position as tiebreak; every other member stands at exactly its natural, so every seam sits on a content boundary and the one stretch of empty space is at the foot of the card that will grow into it first. A member with an infinite natural (Overview) keeps the ladder from reaching this stage at all. Spreading (`[F05]`) is rejected because it hands each card space it did not ask for, and when one card then grows the settle must claw that space back across every seam instead of moving one. Fit's two edge regimes get names in the doctrine — **squeeze** when the naturals exceed the run and members are held below natural in reverse greed order, **slack** when they fall short and this rule applies — because fit is the layout that has edges. They are not user-facing words; the user's words are Fit and Flow (`[B09]`).

**[B07] Fit keeps one derived exception: floors that do not fit the run stand as flow until they do.** Fit cannot be honoured below the floors, and inventing a third geometry for that case would be the count-based rule again with a different count. So the previous brief's `[B01]` is demoted from *the* rule to fit's fallback, and the standing stays derived and visible: a fit place whose floors overflow *reads* as flowing on every face until a member leaves or the window grows. The band does the same when cards will not fit at their floors.

**[B08] In flow a member's height is its own, and a stream's natural is the run.** Height is `max(floor, natural · weight)`, where weight is the multiplier the user's drags stored — the same meaning today's overflow gives it over comfort, moved up one tier because flow's promise is natural. A member with an infinite natural (a stream) stands at the run: one screen of stream, scrolled to, which is what a stream in a scrolling strip should be. When the strip is shorter than the run — three small cards on a tall display — the members pin from the run's top and the remainder is **vacancy** at the rail's foot, honest and empty, on `FlowVacancy`'s precedent; a drop there appends. Greed ranks play no part in flow.

**[B09] The choice is Fit | Flow on every face, and every face says which thing scrolls.** The deck's LAYOUT row and its caption stay exactly as they are — they are about the band. The place's choice wears the same two words because it is the same choice, and giving it a second vocabulary (slack | squeeze, fit | scroll) would make one idea look like two. It is kept apart from the deck's by *where* it appears and *what it names*: (a) it is offered per place — on the rail's own mark under the drawing and in the rail's own badge menu — never in the deck-wide rows, so the two choices are never side by side in one control; (b) the place mark reads on the doctrine's own rule, what is then what the press does — `Split · Fit` / `Split · Flow` with the press stated — and the plan caption's tail says which thing scrolls, `"— the deck scrolls"` for the band and `"— the right rail scrolls"` for a flowing rail, both when both; (c) the miniature draws the difference: a fit rail as a division with seams, a flow rail as a strip that runs off the field, which is the drawing the overflow standing already has. Exact copy is settled with the card on screen, under those three rules.

**[B10] The doors are the rail's existing three, extended.** The Layout card's per-place mark gains the layout beside the mode (`[F10]`'s `layout-places.tsx`, dispatching `SET_RAIL_LAYOUT` / `SET_COLUMN_LAYOUT`); the stack badge menu gains a checked Fit / Flow pair under "Split Vertically" alongside "Equalize Heights"; the seam's double-click equalize means *equal discretionary shares* in fit as before and *clear the weights so every member stands at natural* in flow. No new chrome and no new surface; the arrangement of a place is decided where a place's arrangement has always been decided.

**[B11] Greed ranks are re-tuned so `[B06]`'s order is a decision, not a tie.** `[F06]`'s ranks cannot choose between Cards and Layout. Fixed-content cards (Layout) rank last of the six; list cards rank by how fast their content grows, which puts Jots ahead of Cards and Tripwires; Overview stays at 1 as the only stream. The comfort stage reads the same ranks and moves the same way — a growing list reaches comfort before a fixed picture does, which is right. Numbers set with the cards on screen.

**[B12] The inverse and the drag bounds follow the layout, and the census proves both round trips.** `placeSharesFromHeights` and `seamDragBounds` take the layout rather than inferring a standing. Fit: regime B (weights above natural) is replaced — heights matching `[B06]`'s greedy default return the empty record, and a hand's drag past natural stores weights that override the default for that place. Flow: the inverse is `h_i / natural_i` (a stream reads its run as its natural for this purpose). The census (`layout-imposer-heights-census.test.ts`) gains a layout axis and asserts per row: fit fills the run exactly; flow puts every member at `natural · weight` (or floor); allocate ∘ inverse = identity on every reachable height in both. It must stay green through the change, not be weakened.

### The check

**[B13] A real-app check pins each card's declared natural to its actual content, and it lands first, red.** For each sidebar card, once at its declared natural on a real rail, its scroll container satisfies `scrollHeight ≤ clientHeight + 1` — the previous brief's `[B02]` definition of natural, tested as stated, which `[F04]` shows does not exist. It lives in `at0542` beside the ceiling assertion, which stays: the ceiling proves the allocator obeys the declaration, this proves the declaration is true. Written against today's constants it fails on Layout and Cards before any number moves, so a corrected constant lands as a fact rather than a fresh guess.

## Open Questions {#open-questions}

- **Whether a flow seam drag is zero-sum.** Today's overflow drag grows one member and shrinks its neighbour so the strip's length holds and the scroll position does not move under the hand. In flow a member's height is its own, so the honest drag resizes only the member above the seam and lets the strip lengthen; the boundary stays under the hand either way, and the members below shift. The recommendation is the non-zero-sum drag, because zero-sum would make lengthening one card cost its neighbour a height the neighbour declared it needs. Settle in the app: it is a feel question, and it is the one place the machine changes rather than only the meaning.
- **Whether comfort for a list card should be a row count at all.** "Three rows read as a list" has not been tested with the ladder running. If the comfort stage never binds in practice — runs are tall, floors are 240 — comfort may equal floor for every list and only natural matters. Settle on a laptop-height rail with all six cards open in fit, the one case where comfort decides anything.

## Non-goals {#non-goals}

- **Measuring content height from the DOM.** Still the metric loop the previous brief's `[B03]` refused. Every appetite is a pure function of state; `[B13]` measures the DOM in a *test*, once, to check the declaration, which is the one place measuring is right.
- **A deck-wide vertical layout.** Considered: one Fit | Flow for every rail and column at once, on the LAYOUT row beside the band's. Rejected because a place's arrangement is per place already (mode, order, shares) and because it would put the two choices in one row where they would be confused for one. The default is per place and is fit.
- **A third vertical geometry for floors that do not fit.** Rejected by `[B07]`: fit falls back to flow, as the band does.
- **Spreading fit's slack by weight, with corrected naturals.** Considered and rejected on `[B06]`'s argument about growth, and because the user has said the empty-space-everywhere picture is not what they want.
- **Renaming the two words for the vertical axis.** `[B09]`: same choice, same words, distinguished by place and by the noun that scrolls.
- **A card-level collapse to the title bar.** Still its own feature.
- **Re-serializing offsets, heights or appetites.** Only the new `layout` field joins the blob, additive-optional like `mode`.

## Exit {#exit}

**An arc.** The shape, in the order it must land:

1. **The natural check, red** (`[B13]`) — per-card `scrollHeight ≤ clientHeight + 1` at declared natural in `at0542`, failing on Layout and Cards against today's constants.
2. **Appetites from rows** (`[B01]`–`[B03]`) — Layout from caption, drawing, preset strip and `3 + sidebars.length` rows; Cards by row kind; Jots, Arcs and Tripwires re-derived from their own rules. The check goes green. This alone puts Layout on screen in the rail the user is looking at, under either layout.
3. **The layout as state** (`[B05]`) — `PlaceLayout`, the two arrangement fields, the parsers, the two actions, and `allocatePlaceHeights` taking the layout with the floors test becoming fit's fallback (`[B07]`). Behaviour unchanged at this step for a blob that carries no `layout`.
4. **Fit's tail and flow's heights** (`[B06]`, `[B08]`, `[B11]`, `[B12]`) — the greedy slack rule, flow at `natural · weight` with a stream at the run and vacancy below a short strip, the re-tuned ranks, the layout-aware inverse and bounds, and the census gaining its layout axis and staying green.
5. **The doors and the faces** (`[B09]`, `[B10]`) — the place mark, the badge menu pair, equalize's flow meaning, the caption's tail, and the miniature drawing a flow rail as a strip. The doctrine paragraph "the overflow rule belongs to the place" in `tuglaws/pane-model.md` becomes "the layout belongs to the place," with squeeze and slack named as fit's edges.

Step 1 must precede step 2 or the corrected constants are as unproven as the ones they replace. Step 2 must precede step 4, since flow at a wrong natural is today's clipped Layout wearing a strip. Step 3 is the pivot the rest turn on. Step 5 is separable and could be its own arc if the first four run long, but the choice is not real until it lands.
