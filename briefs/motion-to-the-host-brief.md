# Motion to the host: beat plans, Core Animation on the bodies, the floor in the host, and the bar read on the window

**Purpose:** With a card's content in its own process, the one thing left on the shell's thread during a settle is the shell's own chrome, and the one thing that can still stall a body's motion is the shell deciding where it goes. This brief moves the bodies' motion to the host: the shell plans a settle as it does today and posts each body's beats to the host, which runs them as Core Animation on the body's container, anchored to the same clock as the shell's chrome beats. The grounding stage's interim, bodies lidded and hidden under a moving chrome, gives way to bodies that move; the floor the shell paints around every body moves to the host so no hole has to follow a body per frame; and the asks' bar is read on the window's frames by colour, not on one document's rects.

Stage 3 of `briefs/per-card-web-views-program-brief.md`. Amended 2026-10-14 against `briefs/body-view-grounding-brief.md`'s findings; lands behind the body switch (grounding [B07]) and flips nothing on.

---

## Purpose {#purpose}

The asks: a new card, a fold, a column move, a flow slide, a slot, a bullseye, a rail, a fit, with no gap over one display period from the first frame to the land and a one-frame land, with three or four sessions streaming. After grounding, every slide hides the body under a lid at the beat's start and shows it at the land, coherent but not motion. After this stage the bodies move.

## Evidence {#evidence}

**[F01] The settle engine already plans before it moves, and now tells the placer which frames will move.** `useSettleEngine` (`components/chrome/settle-engine.ts:461`) runs the beats `depart`, `room`, `shrink`, `move`, `grow`, `arrive` and the land from a plan (`settle-plan.ts`, `lib/pane-flip.ts`); the Last pass reads once and writes after (stage 6 [B03], `lib/set-up-apply.ts`). Pane frames animate by FLIP `transform`, width and height tweens, and opacity through `TugAnimator` WAAPI. Since grounding, the Last pass and the flow prelaunch call `bodyHideUnder(frames)` (`settle-engine.ts:2296, 3701` → `lib/body-placement.ts`) with the frames their beats will carry, and the placer lids those bodies and closes their holes before the first frame paints; the land's measurement shows them again. Those two calls are where a beat plan is posted instead. **(verified, read)**

**[F02] The lid, the hole and the order the grounding stage learned.** The shell stands above every body, so whatever it paints over a body's rect hides the body. The shell paints a lid over a body card's content box whenever the body is not shown; the floor (`[data-deck-canvas-floor]`, the canvas colour and grid the page no longer paints under `html[data-body-host]`, `globals.css:83-94`) is cut by an even-odd `clip-path` with a hole at every shown body; every pane behind a visible body wears the same cut (`--tugx-pane-cut`, [F16] of grounding). Order is load-bearing: lid and hole in the commit, the host told on the far side of the shell's paint, because the host's transaction lands at once while the shell's frame is a paint away (grounding [F15]). The container fade is retired: a fade through a hole is the window's background, which is the canvas colour (grounding [F12]). **(verified, read and read on the real binary)**

**[F03] Per-frame cuts in the shell repaint the screen and lag the chrome.** Grounding [F14]: with the floor's `clip-path` re-cut on every frame of a drag, the body the host had already moved led the chrome by 36 px, because the shell's frame commit was held back by the repaint; a compositing layer on the floor (`will-change: transform`) brought the lead inside two frames. Stage 6 found `clip-path` animation is not composited on this WebKit (`at0736`). A hole that must follow a body on every frame of a host-driven motion is therefore a style recalc and a repaint per frame in the shell, on a document that still holds every non-Session card until stage 5 (the Overview's parked twin alone 8k elements). **(verified, measured on the harness)**

**[F04] One frame of pipeline latency between the host and the shell, in both directions.** Grounding [F14]: in the drop's landing spring the body led the chrome by 35 px on one frame, "one frame of pipeline latency between the host's transaction and the shell's layer-tree commit, which the shell cannot close from its side." [F15]: the window server runs a frame or two behind the shell's own record, more on bullseye's heavy first commit. The program brief's open question about skew has a measured lower bound. **(verified, measured on the harness)**

**[F05] The ingest hold is the shell's.** `TugConnection` parks ingest batches while the motion gate is closed (`connection.ts:478, 500-525`), capped by the Worker; the hold rides `RENDER_INPUT`, per page since stage 1. A body's stream has no reason to stop for the shell's settle once the shell's thread is not drawing it. **(verified, read)**

**[F06] What the host has.** `BodyViewController.swift`: `place` sets container frames in a `CATransaction` with actions disabled (`:337-363`), rounds the bottom corners (`:255, :348`), sorts containers in place only when the order changed (`sortContainers`, `:20`); `HostFrameMeter.swift` is the display-link instrument the spike built, reading a container's presentation layer per tick. The canvas colour already reaches the host through `setTheme` (`MainWindow.swift:1337`). **(verified, read)**

**[F07] How a row is read.** Grounding [F13]–[F15], [F21] and the `composite-lead-not-dom-rects` memory: a bar judged inside a DOM rect misses the shell's own paint lag; chrome and body edges are read by colour in a composite window capture (the surface calibrated from a standing body's composite, the lid's colour exact, the floor's grid read as the dim lattice near the canvas colour); a frame is judged in motion only when the chrome's rect moved in two consecutive samples and the capture differs from the standing deck; the session body's own page is near the canvas colour over much of its height and an unfocused body recedes, so "the body is there" cannot be read by colour, while the lid can. **(verified, read from the record)**

**[F08] Stage 6's numbers are the before.** First frame after a fold's commit 77–83 ms on the harness; the height crossing's frames are style and compositing in any shape in one document; fold in-motion 28–42 ms and land 28–42 ms on the user's aged deck; ⌘N set-up 141–416 ms. `tuglaws/animation-doctrine.md` [D9] records the height crossing as a standing hit with the clip and containment shapes benched and failed. **(verified, read from the record)**

**[F09] Core Animation runs on the render server.** A `CABasicAnimation` or `CAKeyframeAnimation` on a layer's `position`, `bounds` or `transform` is committed once and interpolated at display rate independent of the committing process's main thread; `beginTime` is in `CACurrentMediaTime()` terms. The spike measured it: under a 3 s synchronous stall of the shell, a container's translate advanced on 180 of 180 display-link ticks (grounding [F03]). **(verified, measured)**

## Decisions {#decisions}

**[B01] The floor moves to the host, and the shell paints no canvas at all.** A host layer beneath every body draws the canvas colour and the grid, from the theme colour `setTheme` already delivers and the grid tokens the shell posts once per theme and zoom; the shell's floor element and its even-odd cut go. This is the precondition for motion: with no floor to cut, nothing in the shell has to follow a body per frame, and [F03]'s repaint has no object. The window's own background stays the canvas colour, so the window behind an open hole and the floor are one colour, which is what the lid already relies on.

**[B02] The shell plans; the host moves the bodies.** The settle engine keeps every pass it has. Where it calls `bodyHideUnder` today, in the Last pass and the flow prelaunch, it posts a `beatPlan` to the host instead: for every body a beat will carry, an ordered list of beats, each with the container's start and end rect, start and end opacity, duration, easing (the curve names the chrome uses, resolved by the host to `CAMediaTimingFunction` or a keyframe list) and one absolute start time. The host builds the animations on each container's layer (`position` and `bounds` for a move or a crossing, `opacity` for depart and arrive), commits them in one transaction with the model values set to the landing rect so nothing snaps when they are removed, and reports `landed` per body when the last animation's end has passed. The lid stays off a moving body: a body that moves is shown throughout, and the lid and the commit-time hide keep their jobs for arrivals, departures, folds and parks, exactly as grounding built them.

**[B03] One clock, with a calibrated offset.** The shell computes each beat's start time on `performance.now()`, converts through `performance.timeOrigin` to wall time, and the host converts wall time to `CACurrentMediaTime()`, re-anchored on every wake; every WAAPI chrome beat starts with an explicit `startTime` and every CA body beat with the matching `beginTime` plus an offset the host owns. [F04] says the offset is about one display period in the host's favour; the stage's first reading measures the skew between a chrome edge and its body's edge by colour, frame by frame, with the offset at zero, then sets the offset to the measured median and reads again. The offset is a setting the record carries, and the reading says what it is on a 60 Hz and a 120 Hz display.

**[B04] The back-pane cut follows the plan, not the frame.** A pane behind a body that will move keeps its cut, but the cut animates as WAAPI `clip-path` keyframes computed from the same beat plan, started on the same clock, so the shell writes it once and the engine interpolates; grounding's cut was static and this is its moving form. Because `clip-path` is not composited on this WebKit the shell pays a style recalc per frame for each such pane, which is a cost only where a body crosses a back pane (overlap in a stack, fit bands too narrow, bullseye). The reading says what it costs on a deck with the Overview still in the document; if it is a frame, the fallback is a cut over the swept union of First and Last for the motion's length, which over-cuts the back pane's chrome where the body will pass. *Revised 2026-10-14 from `row-16-back-pane-cut`:* because the cut is the main thread's and the body and chrome are the compositor's, a hole keyed exactly to the body stood up to a frame off it, and the Text card behind showed 8–20 % ink inside the body on the first four frames of a 974 px slide; each keyframe's hole is therefore the body's rect swept one display period either side of its sample (same commands, one rectangle per front), which keeps the pane off the body whichever way the two clocks differ, at the cost of a sliver of what lies beneath beside the body's edge, a period's travel at most.

**[B05] Set-up-and-go, read for a body.** A body's box is set once, before the first frame: for a crossing the host sets the body view's final frame inside its container at the commit, and the body pays one layout in its own process while the container still stands at First; the beat then translates and clips the container (`bounds` with `masksToBounds`, the bottom corners rounded as today), which is [D9]'s new shape for the division, on the host's layer, where a bounds animation costs no layout anywhere. The still-crossing hold, `registerCrossingInterior`, `resize-episode` and the crossing CustomEvents do nothing for a body; one bus message, `crossing {phase: begin | land, size}`, tells the body its final size is coming and when it has landed, and `smart-scroll`'s pin and restore run in the body on it. The fold keeps grounding's shape (the body hidden at the commit, shown at the land) unless the fold's reading says the crossing beat on the container reads better; the fold is read both ways and the record says which.

**[B06] The ingest hold is retired for bodies.** A body streams through a settle; its frames are its own process's. The shell's gate holds only the shell's own batches. The one-period bar is read with three sessions streaming into three bodies, which is the condition the program was opened on.

**[B07] Parked workspaces hide their bodies, and a hidden body costs nothing.** As grounding built it: `activate-space` posts the arrangement, every departing body is lidded and hidden at the commit, every arriving one placed on its own placeholder and shown under the lid; nothing here changes, and a workspace switch stays a show-and-hide rather than a motion.

**[B08] The bar is read on the window, by colour.** The host's display-link meter counts the window's frames and gaps across a settle and reports them beside the shell's gap rows; `tugtool deck motion settle` prints both and labels the body beats by card; the per-gesture files (`at0696`–`at0708`) and new `body-grounding` rows read the window's row as the bar, judging motion by chrome and body edges in composite captures as [F07] says, never by DOM rects. The arrival stays grounding's: lid, `painted`, one-step show, `shown`, a 200 ms lid lift, read as row 1 reads it. A reading of record for ⌘N, fold, slot, split, flow slide, bullseye and rails on the user's aged deck with three streams, before and after, goes into the program record with the honesty rules in force, on a dev instance with the switch on until the user flips it.

*As read (2026-10-14, `body-grounding/record-reading.test.ts`; the numbers are in the program record's stage 3 reading).* Before and after, with three bodies streaming, ⌘N, fold, slot, split, flow slide, bullseye and rails each missed no window tick in any reading, and the worst tick gap was one period. After, every body a gesture moved rode the host's beats, advancing on every tick it was active and landing on time. The shell's own rows did not move with the arc, beyond bullseye's land falling from 27–29 ms to 21–22. That is the right result: before, bodies sat under a lid for the motion, so the shell's frames were the whole of what moved, and after, the bodies move on the host's clock without adding to them. Two things departed from the decision's letter, each for the reason it was written. The reading was taken in the harness, with stub turns replayed through tugcast's real feed, because three real turns on a dev instance need `claude` turns that only the user can start. Each body's stream was checked in its own page, because the verb's `--streaming` count reads the shell's document. The user's aged deck is read when the user flips the switch. The crossing's lead under load, the one bar this reading left owed, is met: see the open questions below.

**[B09] The chrome follows if the skew says so.** If [B03]'s reading shows the chrome beats and body beats a frame apart under load after the offset is set, the pane's title bar and tab strip become host-drawn strips above the body (an `NSView` with the shell's tokens; never a second web view per card), and the shell keeps columns, rails and overlays. An escalation the reading opens, not a plan.

## Open Questions {#open-questions}

- **What is the skew after the offset?** [B03]; the first reading of the stage. **Answered 2026-10-14 (`row-15-skew.test.ts`): under a quarter of a display period, with the body a little ahead of the chrome; the offset ships at 0.2 periods.** Read on a flow slide out and back (193 px each way, fastest step 26–29 px per frame) by a magenta stripe fixed to the body page's left edge against the title bar's left edge, on composite frames judged in motion, at 60 Hz on a Studio Display (the only rate attached to this machine; 120 Hz was not available to read). At an offset of 0 the body leads its chrome by a median of 0.20 periods (0.19–0.26 across four readings), at most 7 px or 0.49 periods. With `shippedBeatOffsetPeriods = 0.2` the median is 0.04–0.08 periods and the worst 3 px at rest; with three bodies streaming through the slides it is −0.04 periods and the worst 2 px. The edges never stand more than 0.75 periods of travel apart, so [B09]'s trigger (a frame apart under load, after the offset) is not met and [B09] stays closed. The shell's wall clock agrees with `Date.now()` to the millisecond on a release page up 57 minutes (Risk R01's drift reads −1 to 0 ms, the integer ms of `Date.now()`), so `shellWallMs` keeps `performance.timeOrigin + t`; no sleep and wake fell inside that page's life. One thing the reading left open: at an offset of 0, the first slide of a launch shows no stripe on its first 7–8 moving frames in every reading, while the same slide at 0.2 and every second slide read in full, so the early curve of that one slide is missing from the median rather than read into it.
- **What does the moving back-pane cut cost with the Overview still in the shell's document?** [B04]; if a frame, the swept-union fallback, and a note to stage 5 that the Overview goes first. **Answered 2026-10-14 (`row-16-back-pane-cut.test.ts`): nothing the shell's gap rows can see, so the animated cut stays the default.** A body card carried 974 px across a Text card of block glyphs behind it in a three-up fit, and 567 px with the Overview open in a left rail: the shell recorded no settle gap at all over either motion (0 judder, 0 lead), with the animated cut and with the swept union forced alike, at 60 Hz in the harness's build of this tree. Read inside the body, the covered strip carries at most 1.4 % ink on the bare band (the body's own text) and 3.7 % with the Overview, on the first moving frame, all of it at the strip's leading edge, against a 5 % bar. So no note to stage 5 is owed on the cut's account. The reading was taken in the harness rather than on a dev instance: the deck it needs (a body card, a Text card overlapping it, the Overview in a rail) is the harness's seeded state, and the harness app is the same binary.
- **Does a container `bounds` crossing show the body's bottom edge cleanly, or does the body need its own inner clip?** Looked at on the real binary with the fold read both ways ([B05]).
  - **Answered (2026-10-14).** The fold was read both ways on the real binary. Grounding's shape — hide the body at the commit, show it at the land — keeps row 9 green: the content box is the lid on every frame in motion. Read as a crossing, the shape failed. A probe let the planner carry the folding body to a zero-height landing, with the page held and the container clipping it. Then a placement cancelled the body's beats one frame into the fold, the container snapped to the folded rect, and the closing pane's content box showed the floor grid (12–77 % surface, up to 80 % canvas over four frames in motion). A folded card's content box has no body to show, so nothing can fill it. **The fold keeps grounding's hide-and-show.** For every other crossing, the page takes its landing size once the body has pinned its scrollers and acknowledged. The container is flipped, so the page's top-left stays put, and it carries no inner clip view. The bottom edge needed one thing the decision did not name. Through a shrink the container is larger than the page, so for the crossing the container wears the page's own surface, sent as sRGB bytes with `crossingReady`. The strip the page does not reach is then the body's surface, never the floor. `html` computes transparent in a body page, so the colour is read off the box that paints it. `crossingReady` comes one bus round trip after the plan is posted, which is 2–11 ms after the first beat that moves the box, inside one display period at 60 Hz. Risk R02 foresaw this: until it comes, the container clips the page at its old size over the surface. The bar is therefore one display period, with the margin noted, rather than strictly before the first moving beat. Read in `row-17-crossing` and `row-04-slide`.
  - **Reading against that bar (2026-10-14, the window-bar step).** It does not hold under load. Across five runs of `row-17-crossing` on the arc's tree, `crossingReady` came 17.0, 17.9, 18.7 and 20.5 ms after the first moving beat (bullseye in and out) against a 16.7 ms period: red in every batch run, green twice and red once (17.0 ms) alone. The 2–11 ms above was an unloaded reading. The cost is the one R02 named, the page clipped at its old size over its own surface for one frame more than the bar allows. It is a defect of the crossing, not of the bar, and it is fixed in the arc's integration step: the `card-crossing` begin reaches the body over the bus, and the round trip that has to fit inside one period is the thing to shorten, never the bar to widen.
  - **Fixed (2026-10-14, the integration step).** The begin no longer goes over the bus. The host has the plan in hand, so it calls into the body's page itself (`onBodyCrossing`) in the task it runs the plan in, and it says `land` at the beats' land or cancel. The body pins and acknowledges in one hop there and one back, and the `card-crossing` bus kind is gone. In a 24-file run of the grounding bar, `crossingReady` came 9.6–12.9 ms *before* the first moving beat on every crossing (bullseye in and out). Before, it came 17.0–20.5 ms after it. The bar stays one display period, and it is met with room to spare.
- **Does the host's grid match the CSS grid to the pixel across zoom?** [B01]; read by composite against the in-document deck at three zoom levels (the tiled-dot-grid rounding is the known hazard). **Answered 2026-10-14 (`floor-host.test.ts`): yes.** On a 2× display, over a 240×160 pt crop of bare canvas, every line of the page's grid has one of the host's within 0 device px at zoom 0.8 and 1.0 and within 0.5 device px at 1.25, none missing and none extra, and the canvas colour is identical (`rgb(22, 24, 29)` in Ironclad). `FloorGrid.roundToDevicePixel` rounds to the nearest device pixel and needed no change. One thing the reading taught: the shell posts its grid when the deck mounts, which can land after the window's reveal fade has begun, so the host floor joins that fade on its own clock rather than appearing un-faded under a half-faded shell.

## Non-goals {#non-goals}

- **Moving the plan to the host.** The shell stays the authority on arrangement; the host never decides where a body goes.
- **A third bench of height by translation inside a document.** Ruled; the division moves to the host's layer.
- **Changing the beat vocabulary or the curves.** The doctrine's beats and timings stand; they are executed in two places.
- **Host-side drag.** Grounding's shell-driven drag (`bodyFollowFrame`) lands the body frame for frame and stays.
- **Flipping the switch.** Lands behind it; the default changes only by the program brief's decision.

It joins when ⌘N, fold, slot, split, flow slide, bullseye and rails hold the asks' bar on the window on a dev instance with three streams, written into the program record, or when the reading says why not.
