# Focus, keys and the edges: first responder across views, chords, Tab at a card's edge, and what draws over what

**Purpose:** The deck arbitrates focus, chords, menus, undo, clipboard, selection paint and overlays inside one document. With a body per card, macOS has one first responder per window and each body is a view that can be it. The grounding stage built the arbiter, the chord path, the menu merge, undo, lookup and dictation per view, and read them green on the real binary. What is left is the edges: Tab at a card's end, the surfaces a body raises that must draw over the deck, the surfaces it raises that must sit on its own transcript, a file dragged into a body, the hole a dragged card leaves in the card it crossed, the deck-wide facts a body still misses, and VoiceOver's order.

Stage 4 of `briefs/per-card-web-views-program-brief.md`. Amended 2026-10-15 against stage 2a (`afc3dd3a1`) and stage 3 (`4cb4b8974`), and against four defects the user found on their deck with the switch on.

## Purpose {#purpose}

The user: make first responder "work as it does now, but on a modified/more complex tech base." Everything a user does with the keyboard and the pointer today keeps working, from any card, with the same chords and the same menus.

The user has run bodies on their daily deck since 2026-10-14 (`dev.tugapp.deck/bodies` set in their release-main bank at 13:18) and reports three things that do not work as they did:

- "Compacting in a session/arc should position the sheet at the bottom of the transcript, not the top." The screenshot shows the Compacting cover standing at the top of a body card's transcript.
- "Drag and drop of images into a prompt-entry component does not work."
- "When cards animate, they leave holes behind them as they move." Three screenshots of one gesture: a body card dragged out of a stack across an in-document Text card into an empty slot. After the landing, the Text card is cut off below the dragged card's path, with the floor's grid showing where its content was.
- "The borders of session cards have a spuriously bright/incorrect border on the left and right." The screenshot shows two body session cards side by side: below each masthead, a light strip about two device pixels wide runs down both sides of the content box to the card's bottom, brighter than the pane's own 1 px border, which the masthead's sides wear at its normal colour. The strip stops where the body stops.

## Evidence {#evidence}

**[F01] The arbiter is built, and it is the shell's policy with the host following.** `FocusManager` and the first-responder flip stay the one decision of which card is key; on every change the shell posts `bodyFocus {cardId}` to the host (`lib/body-shell.ts:201`), which makes that body's web view or the shell's first responder (`BodyViewController.swift:881-886`). A page that does not hold the keyboard writes no DOM focus: the keyboard yield (`lib/keyboard-yield.ts`) is consulted before every focus write the focus engine makes (`focus-manager.ts:1186, 1193, 3040, 3118`); a body yields unless its card is the first responder (`body/body-main.ts:171, 301`). A click in a body reaches the body's page through the host's hit test (`ClickThroughWebView.hitTest` returns nil where `passesThrough` says, `MainWindow.swift:289-293`; `BodyHitRegion`, `BodyGeometry.swift:28-43`) and the body's `pointerdown` asks the shell to activate its card over the bus. Row 7 reads it green on the real binary: click, Focus Card chord, the shell's and the body's own surfaces taking and returning focus, the close that hands focus on (`tests/app-test/body-grounding/row-07-focus.test.ts`). **(verified, read and run)**

**[F02] Chords and menus are built.** A body runs the same keydown funnel over its own registry scope; what its chain does not answer is forwarded to the shell as `card-advice dispatch`, with Cancel the one command declined rather than forwarded so an Escape reaches the surface that owns it ([F17] of grounding; `body-main.ts:322-341`). The shell forwards a `first-responder` or `key-card` command to the key body unless a shell surface holds the chain ahead (`lib/body-command-route.ts`). A body posts the gates of its card's items, projected from the shell's `DeckFacts.menu` rather than its one-card deck (`host-menu-state.ts`, `applyShellMenuFacts`; grounding [F19]). Row 5 reads every keyboard-file chord from a body and from the shell through the session tap; row 6 performs 106 File, Edit, View, Go and Window items with a body first responder (grounding [F18]). The question the original brief left open, whether WKWebView eats a key equivalent it does not handle before AppKit's menu sees it, is answered by those rows: it does not, on the app's own path. **(verified, run)**

**[F03] Edit, undo, lookup and dictation follow the first responder.** `editingUndoManager()` returns the key body's web view's undo manager when the first responder is inside one (`MainWindow.swift:1293-1300`); a message is answered into the view that sent it (`:2201-2202`), so `showDefinition` and the clipboard reply into the asking body; dictation targets the view that sent `start` (`:423-425, 1691, 2523`) and the body installs the receiver (`body-main.ts:166`). Row 12 lands text and a PNG from the real pasteboard in a body's composer. **(verified, read and run)**

**[F04] Deck facts reach a body, with gaps.** `deck-facts` carries theme, zoom, keyboard access, app-active and the menu projection, and the body applies each (`body-main.ts:201-214`; `tugproto/src/bus.ts:91-106`). Two facts are not sent: `modifierLatch` is always the empty list (`body-shell.ts:102`), and focus-ring modality is read by each page from tugbank at boot rather than followed (`body-main.ts:455`). Keyboard access is applied unpersisted, which is right. **(verified, read)**

**[F05] Tab never leaves a body.** `focusNext`/`focusPrevious` wrap inside the key card (`focus-manager.ts:2043-2051`), and a body's document is one card, so Tab from the last stop in a body returns to its first; the shell's `advanceKeyViewFocus` (`:4619`) is never asked. No `focus-exit` exists on the bus (`grep` over `tugdeck/src`, `tugapp/Sources`). **(verified, read)**

**[F06] Overlays: a body has its own overlay root, so everything a body raises is clipped to its card.** `BodyRoot` mounts a `CanvasOverlayRoot` and a `TugTooltipProvider` of its own (`body/body-root.tsx:30, 125, 140`); a popover, context menu, tooltip or dialog raised in a body portals there and cannot draw past the body's view. The shell claims the pointer over its own overlays (`lib/pointer-claim.ts`; `pointerClaim` to the host) so a shell surface over a body takes the click. The picker-to-body handoff (row 12) and the shell's Close popover over a body (row 7) are the two shell-drawn cases read green. Which other deck-scope surfaces a body can raise today, and what each does when clipped, has not been enumerated. **(verified, read; the enumeration is owed)**

**[F07] The compaction cover anchors to a rest line that a body's sheet host does not resolve.** The cover is raised with `bottomAnchorSelector: MODAL_REST_LINE` (`.session-view-slot`; `cards/session-compaction-run.tsx:341`, `cards/modal-rest-line.ts:42`), and `TugSheet` resolves it with `cardEl.querySelector(selector)` where `cardEl` is the `TugPanePortalContext` (`tug-sheet.tsx:1274, 1494-1499`); a selector that matches nothing "leaves the default top anchor in place." In a body the portal context is the bridge's element, not the pane's, and the screenshot shows the cover at the top of the transcript with the rest line's bottom-anchored rise gone. The most likely cause is the context element: either it is not an ancestor of the view slot, or the clip's geometry is measured against a canvas the body does not have. **(inferred from the code and the screenshot; the arc confirms by reading `cardEl` in a body)**

**[F08] A file dragged into a body is dropped on the shell.** The deck's drop surfaces are DOM listeners inside the Session card (`tugways/use-composer-drop.ts`, `cards/use-session-card-drop.ts`, `tug-text-editor/drop-extension.ts`), which in a body live in the body's page. The host registers no drag types and overrides no `NSDraggingDestination` method on any view (`grep` over `tugapp/Sources`: only `mouseDragged`). The shell's web view covers the window; AppKit delivers a drag to the registered destination under the pointer, which is the shell's WKWebView, whose page has a transparent placeholder where the body shows and no listener there that accepts `Files`, so the drag reads as refused. `pointer-claim.ts` says it plainly: "A drag needs no claim: AppKit keeps sending a drag to the view that took its `mouseDown`", which is true of a drag that began in the window and false of one arriving from Finder. **(the mechanism is read; the delivery to the shell is inferred and is the arc's first reading)**

**[F09] The back-pane cut survives the gesture that made it.** A pane standing behind a body card wears an even-odd `clip-path` with a hole at every front body's rect, rewritten by the placer on every placement (`lib/body-placement.ts:1038-1051`, `cutPanes`; grounding [F16]); a shell-driven drag re-posts the dragged body at its rect on every pointer frame and every frame of the drop's landing tween (`bodyFollowFrame`, `:1234`; grounding [B03]). The user's screenshots show the in-document Text card the body crossed still cut after the landing, with the floor's grid where its content was, and the hole's top edge at the height the dragged card passed. `cutPanes` rewrites every pane in `paneBoxes` from the current placement, so a hole that stays means either the landed placement still lists the body as in front of that pane, or the pane's box or the cut it holds is stale after the drag's final re-post. Stage 3's row 16 reads a settle-driven crossing, not a drag, and no row reads the cut after a drag ends. **(the symptom is the user's; the cause is read to two candidates, not proven)**

**[F10] Nothing orders bodies for VoiceOver.** `BodyViewController.swift` mentions accessibility nowhere; the window's accessibility children are the views in subview order, which is creation order sorted by stacking (`sortContainers`), not the shell's pane order. **(verified, read)**

**[F11] The selection guard still runs in the shell, over in-document cards only.** `selection-guard.ts` reads every card's Range in the shell's document to paint an inactive selection; a body's selection is in another document and WebKit paints it inactive on its own when the view is not first responder. The guard is referenced from ten shell files (`focus-transfer.ts`, `action-dispatch.ts`, `deck-manager-store.ts`, `card-host.tsx`, the editors). **(verified, read)**

**[F12] A body's side edges are not the pane's border.** The shell measures the placeholder with `getBoundingClientRect()` in zoomed CSS pixels and corrects it for zoom (`lib/body-placement.ts:463-472`), so the rect the host is handed is fractional whenever the deck is zoomed, and the user's deck runs at 80–90 %. The host sets that rect as the container's frame and rounds nothing (`BodyViewController.swift`: the only device-pixel rounding is the floor grid's); Core Animation snaps a fractional frame to device pixels on its own, so the container's edge can land up to half a device pixel past the placeholder's inner edge, under or beside the pane's 1 px border. Inside the page, `html` paints `--tug7-surface-global-primary-normal-content-rest` and `body` and `#deck-container` paint `--tugx-pane-bg` (`body/body-page.css:5-16`); the page's zoom is `transform: scale()` on `#deck-container` sized at `100vw / factor` (`lib/page-zoom-store.ts:103-117`), and a scaled width that does not cover the view's last device column leaves the `html` colour showing there. Both are consistent with a light strip on the sides only, at the body's height only, and neither is proven: the strip's colour against the three tokens, read by composite at zoom 0.8, 0.9 and 1.0, tells them apart. **(the symptom is the user's screenshot; the two causes are read from the code, not measured)**

## Decisions {#decisions}

**[B01] Grounding's arbiter is the arbiter, and this stage adds nothing to it.** The shell decides, posts `bodyFocus`, the host follows, and the keyboard yield keeps every other page's hands off the window's first responder ([F01]). The original brief's `pointerActivate` is the body's `pointerdown` advice as built. Row 7 is the test; it stays green on every row this stage touches. The one addition is a row reading focus across a stage 3 motion: the body that holds the keyboard keeps it through a settle that carries it on the host, and typing during the motion lands in it.

**[B02] Tab at a card's edge hands focus to the shell over the bus.** When a body's `focusNext`/`focusPrevious` would wrap ([F05]), it posts `focus-exit {direction}` instead of wrapping, and the shell's `advanceKeyViewFocus` takes the next stop in deck order, which may be chrome, an in-document card or another body, and the flip follows. Entering a body from the shell by Tab lands on the body's first or last stop by direction, which the shell says in the `card-context` it sends with the flip. The ring is the body's own inside it and the shell's on its chrome, as today; the shell draws no ring over a body.

**[B03] A body's sheets rest where the card's do.** The sheet host in a body resolves `bottomAnchorSelector` and its clip geometry against the body's card element, not the pane's ([F07]). The fix is in the bridge's contexts, so that `TugPanePortalContext` in a body is the element that contains the card's view slot and the sheet's canvas measurement reads the body's viewport; `session-compaction-run.tsx` and `tug-sheet.tsx` do not learn they are in a body. The row reads the Compacting cover's bottom edge against the Z2 row by composite colour in a body, and the history view and the Changes shade beside it, which row 8 already reads.

**[B04] Deck-scope surfaces from a body are drawn by the shell; in-card surfaces stay in-card.** The arc first enumerates every surface a Session card can raise through its overlay root ([F06]) and sorts it: a surface that must draw past the card's bounds (the `ask` dialog, a confirm, the session picker, the command picker's deck-wide form, a popover anchored at the card's edge that opens outward) is requested from the shell on the bus with its anchor rect in viewport coordinates and its content model, and the shell draws it over everything and posts `pointerClaim` while it stands; a surface that fits inside the card (tooltips, context menus, the find bar, the history `TugSheet`, the Changes shade, the compaction cover, the slash-command popup) stays in the body. The sorting is written into the arc's record so stage 5 inherits it for every other card kind.

**[B05] A drag from outside the window reaches the body beneath the pointer.** The host's shell view becomes an `NSDraggingDestination` that forwards: on `draggingEntered`, `draggingUpdated`, `draggingExited` and `performDragOperation`, if the pointer is at a point the hit region passes through ([F01]) and no `pointerClaim` covers it, the call is forwarded to that body's web view, which WebKit turns into the page's own `dragenter`/`dragover`/`drop` with the files on `dataTransfer`; otherwise the shell's view handles it as today. The first reading of the arc is whether AppKit's destination search already consults `hitTest` (then no forwarding is needed and the finding is corrected); the second is a PNG from Finder landing as an attachment tile in a body's composer through the session tap and a real `NSDraggingSession`, never a synthesized DOM drop. A jot dragged from the Jots card into a body's composer is a WebKit-originated drag crossing two web views of one origin and is read in the same row; if WebKit does not carry the custom type across, the jot drag becomes a bus message the way the picker handoff is.

**[B06] A drag leaves no hole.** The back-pane cut is cleared or re-cut from the landed placement when a shell-driven drag ends ([F09]): the placer's final re-post after the drop's landing tween is followed by one measurement of every pane box, and `cutPanes` runs on that, so a pane the body no longer stands in front of wears no hole. The arc reads which of the two candidate causes it is before fixing it, and the row reads the Text card's content by composite colour after a body card is dragged across it and landed elsewhere, on the real binary, and after a drag that is cancelled. The same row reads a stage 3 crossing's cut at its land, so the two paths are held to one assertion.

**[B07] Deck facts are complete.** `modifierLatch` is sent from the shell's latch store and applied to the body's root; focus-ring modality is a fact, not a boot-time read ([F04]); `data-app-active` and the shown workspace are already applied. The body never persists a fact it was sent.

**[B08] Selection paint is WebKit's.** `selection-guard.ts` keeps its job for in-document cards and is told nothing about bodies ([F11]); each body holds its own native selection and WebKit paints it inactive when the view is not first responder. Cross-card selection does not exist, by the user's ruling. The guard retires with the in-document path in stage 5, not here.

**[B09] VoiceOver reads in deck order.** The host sets the window's accessibility children to the shell's pane order, updated with every placement ([F10]); `voiceover-changed` reaches every page. Read with the harness's accessibility inspector on a body and its neighbours.

**[B12] A body card's edges are the pane's border and nothing else.** Whichever of [F12]'s causes the reading names is fixed at its source: a rect posted to the host is snapped to device pixels in the shell, inward, before it is posted, so the container never stands past the placeholder's inner edge; and the body page's `html`, `body` and `#deck-container` paint one token, with the scaled container covering the view at every zoom. The row reads a body card's left and right edges by composite colour against the pane border token and the pane surface token at zoom 0.8, 0.9 and 1.0, on the Debug bundle and the real binary, with the card key and not key, and the same crop on an in-document card beside it as the reference: the two edge profiles must match.

**[B10] Every row takes the app's own path and is read on the real binary.** As grounding ruled ([B04], [B05] there): keys through the session tap with the app active, menu items performed, drags from a real `NSDraggingSession`, effects asserted where they land, frames read by composite colour, and each row also read on a dev instance with the switch on. The rows are `row-20` onward under `tests/app-test/body-grounding/`, one file per row, listed in its README: focus through a motion ([B01]), Tab at the edge both ways ([B02]), the compaction cover and sheets at rest ([B03]), each deck-scope surface and each in-card one ([B04]), a file and a jot dropped into a body ([B05]), the hole after a drag and after a cancel ([B06]), the facts ([B07]), the order ([B09]), the edges at three zooms ([B12]). The keyboard files already in the corpus are not migrated here; they run in the shell as they do, and stage 5's sweep moves them.

**[B11] It lands behind the switch and flips nothing on.** As grounding [B07] says: the bar stays green on every row it touches, these rows are added to it, and the switch's default is the program brief's decision after the user's daily run.

## Non-goals {#non-goals}

- **Re-ordering or re-binding any chord.** The vocabulary and the registry stand.
- **A host-level keymap or a `performKeyEquivalent` override.** Grounding's rows read the page as the gate and AppKit as the menu on the app's own path, and nothing was eaten ([F02]).
- **Host-side card drag.** Grounding's shell-driven drag (`bodyFollowFrame`) lands the body frame for frame and stage 3 kept it; this stage fixes the hole it leaves, not the drag.
- **Cross-document Tab inside a card.** A card is one document.
- **Cross-card text selection.** Ruled out by the user.
- **Retiring `selection-guard.ts` or migrating the shell's keyboard files.** Stage 5's, with the in-document path.
- **Flipping the switch.**
