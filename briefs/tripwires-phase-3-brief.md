# Tripwires phase 3: opening a card on a session that already has an owner

**Purpose:** Phase 3 of `briefs/tripwires-multi-phase-brief.md`. The parent asked for the adoption capability of `briefs/background-session-card-adoption-brief.md` — a card seated on a background session, running or settled, with its history replayed and its live stream attached. Reading the code as it stands, almost all of that is built and shipped: the mid-turn replay the adoption brief called the load-bearing case is the case `runReplay` was written for, and the deck fires it on every fresh binding already. What is not built, and what this phase is actually about, is the **handover**: a trip's session has an owner that closes it the moment it goes quiet, and adopting a session today means watching the engine kill it under you.

---

## Purpose {#purpose}

The parent's Phase 3 reads:

> **Covers:** The adoption brief [B07]: adoption by session id, running or settled, history replayed, live stream attached, idempotent single-seating, working composer, with the Tripwires row as first caller. Settles where adoption lives, which replay path it reuses, the live-attach boundary, and the source of truth for sessions without cards.
>
> **Done when:** an app-test adopts a running trip mid-turn and sees the boundary frame exactly once, and a settled trip opens onto its full transcript.

The parent's [F12] recorded that "the load-bearing case, attaching a card to a mid-turn live session and reconciling replay against the live stream, does not exist." That finding was made by grepping for adoption operations, and it is wrong about the machinery — not about the gesture. The mid-turn replay exists, is request-driven, and has its own shipped design; what does not exist is any way to point it at a session nobody is watching, and any answer to what happens to that session's background owner when somebody starts watching.

The adoption brief predates the tripwire engine in its current form. It speaks of the Lens and of dashes, both retired, and its five "what to settle in the plan" bullets were written against a codebase where the replay question was open. Three of the five are answered below by reading the code. The two that remain are not the ones it expected.

---

## Evidence {#evidence}

**[F01] The mid-turn replay the adoption brief called the load-bearing case is built, shipped, and request-driven.** `runReplay` in `tugcode/src/session.ts` iterates the session's JSONL, adopts an in-flight `ActiveTurn` and re-emits it as a replay snapshot, buffers frames arriving live during the replay window (with an overflow warn line at `dev::replay::live_buffer_overflow`), injects synthetics for journal rows whose text has not reached JSONL yet, holds a re-entrancy guard, installs an early-exit watcher so a claude crash mid-replay emits `replay_complete { claude_exited_during_replay }` rather than racing a `resume_failed`, and closes the bracket with `replay_complete`. Its single trigger is the `request_replay` verb (`tugcode/src/inbound-dispatch.ts:113`); `tugcode/src/main.ts:443` states that cold boot no longer calls it directly and that "replay is request-driven only." Reconciling a live stream against replayed history is the problem that machinery was written to solve. **(verified)**

**[F02] The deck already fires that verb on every fresh card-to-session binding, whatever the session's origin.** `cardServicesStore._reconcile` (`tugdeck/src/lib/card-services-store.ts:287`) diffs `cardSessionBindingStore` against its services map, constructs services for any new `(cardId → tugSessionId)` pair — rebuilding when a card flips to a different session — and dispatches `request_replay` for it. The comment at `:642` records that the old `binding.sessionMode === "resume"` gate was removed deliberately, because "any rebind of the same session needs a replay regardless of the original spawn mode." So a card that acquires a binding to a background session rehydrates its transcript with nothing new written on either side. **(verified)**

**[F03] `fireRestore` is the adoption primitive and already ships.** `tugdeck/src/lib/session-restore.ts:1078` takes `(cardId, tugSessionId, projectDir, connection, display?)`, provisions the spawn tag and line, sends `spawn_session` with `mode=resume`, and registers a restore expectation the card body renders against. `/resume` (`tugdeck/src/components/tugways/cards/resume-sheet.tsx`) and the full session picker's Open both go through it, and its own header describes a resume as "a genuine resume → replay rebuilds the transcript." Nothing in it knows or cares whether the session it names was born on a card. **(verified)**

**[F04] The supervisor's resume arbitration already admits a live headless session, and does not re-spawn it.** A resume is rejected only when all three of (a) the entry is in a not-closed `spawn_state`, (b) the ledger's `card_id` differs from the resuming card, and (c) `held_by_live_client_before` — a live WebSocket client was already holding the session (`agent_supervisor.rs:5057–5093`). That third condition is computed from the `client_sessions` affinity map at `:4895`, and `spawn_headless_session`'s own doc comment says its pipeline is `do_spawn_session`'s "minus everything that belongs to a client: no `client_sessions` affinity row, no `spawn_session_ok` ack, and none of the resume arbitration." So (c) is false for every tripwire session and the resume is admitted. The eager spawn that follows is gated on `spawn_state == SpawnState::Idle` (`:5250`), so a `Live` entry has its `card_id` re-pointed and no second subprocess is launched onto one JSONL. **(verified)**

**[F05] And then the engine kills the session out from under the card.** `SupervisorTripwireSessions::run` calls `close_headless_session` unconditionally the moment `wait_for_session_end` returns (`tripwire_session.rs:247`). `close_headless_session` rebuilds the synthetic card id and hands it to `do_close_session` (`agent_supervisor.rs:5560`), which **never reads that card id for the decision**: it removes the ledger entry, strips the session from every client's affinity set, flips `spawn_state` to `Closed`, and cancels the worker token. An adopted card's session is torn down mid-conversation, its own affinity row swept with everyone else's. The trip then settles `failed` under `unresolved_headline`'s "ended its turn without running the resolution verb" — because `wait_for_session_end` spends its first quiet reading whether the quiet is the tripwire's model finishing or the user's own turn ending. This is the collision the phase exists to resolve, and it is the one thing the adoption brief could not have foreseen. **(verified)**

**[F06] The directory an adopted trip session runs in is disposable by design.** Diagnosis runs in the landing's detached inspection tree under the tripwire scratch root, refcounted across the landing's live trips and removed when the last one settles (`tripwire_tree.rs`); a tree whose sha has no live trip is swept on the engine's start and on its tick. Authoring runs in the tripwire's arc worktree, which `keep_or_discard` discards unless the trip settled `awaiting` or the branch carries rounds (`tripwire.rs:1562`). `fireRestore` wants a `projectDir` and the card shows it; for a trip the honest answer is a path that is scheduled for deletion. **(verified)**

**[F07] The source of truth for cardless sessions exists; what is missing is a word for "cardless."** `SessionLedger::list_sessions_recent(since, until, active_only, limit)` (`session_ledger.rs:3882`) returns every live session with its `card_id` and `project_dir`, and `get(session_id)` answers for one. Every headless session gets a row, because the bridge records on `session_init` like any other. What distinguishes a background session is only its synthetic `card_id`: `TRIPWIRE_CARD_PREFIX` (`"tripwire:"`) appears in exactly two places in the tree, `agent_supervisor.rs:5451` and `:5565`, and **both are writes** — no reader filters on it, no listing excludes it, and no type says a `card_id` might not name a deck card. **(verified)**

**[F08] The Tripwires row holds the id and cannot do anything with it, and could not compose the call if it tried.** `TripwireRow.running_session` reaches the deck and keys a dot and nothing else (parent [F10]). The store's only writes are `setKnobs` over `POST /api/tripwires/<name>` (`tripwires-store.ts:185`), and the deck's action dispatch registers exactly one tripwire action, `toggle-tripwires`, which opens the card (`action-dispatch.ts:555`). `TripRow` carries no worktree and no project dir, so even given a gesture the row could not supply `fireRestore`'s third argument. **(verified)**

**[F09] Double-seating is already refused, but with an error rather than a raise.** Once a real card holds an adopted session, that card *is* a live client with a `client_sessions` row, so a second card's resume meets all three conditions of [F04] and is rejected with `ControlError::CapExceeded { reason: "session_live_elsewhere" }` plus a `SESSION_STATE = errored` broadcast (`agent_supervisor.rs:5085–5093`). Same-card re-adoption succeeds as an ordinary reconnect. The adoption brief's "clicking twice does not make two cards" therefore holds by construction; its "raises that card instead" does not exist, and the second click currently surfaces as a failure. **(verified)**

**[F10] Nothing on either side distinguishes a trip's session from an ordinary one once it is adopted.** The prompt the diagnosis session carries ends in the resolution contract — the session has been told to run `tugtool tripwire resolve <name>` — and that verb is a `tugtool` in another process writing the trip row, which works from any shell the session can reach (Spec S02, `tugtool/src/tripwire.rs`). So an adopted session retains the ability to settle its own trip, and a user who redirects it retains the ability to strand one. **(verified)**

## Decisions {#decisions}

**[B01] Adoption is the resume that already ships, aimed at a session that has no card. No new replay path, no new attach, no read-only transcript view.** [F01] through [F04] are the argument: the replay is built and request-driven, the deck fires it on every fresh binding, `fireRestore` is the primitive, and the supervisor's gate already admits the case. The adoption brief's fear — that a mid-turn attach would need frames ordered against replayed history and that a read-only surface might be the cheaper first step — was answered by the mid-turn-replay work in between. What this phase builds is the *permission* to aim that gesture at a background session and the *handover* that has to accompany it. Revisit only if the replay bracket turns out to behave differently against a session this process did not spawn for a card, which the app-test in the Exit is what would show.

**[B02] A background owner stops owning its session the moment a card adopts it, and the close is what proves it.** [F05] is the defect and this is its shape: `close_headless_session` must refuse to close a session whose entry no longer carries the synthetic card id it was given, and `wait_for_session_end` must stop reading that session's quiet as an answer to the tripwire's question. The check is cheap and exact — the entry's own `card_id` is re-pointed by the resume at `agent_supervisor.rs:5195`, so "is this still mine?" is a field comparison rather than a new piece of state. What the *trip* becomes is a separate question and is left open below; what is decided here is that the engine never destroys a session a user is holding, and never records that user's turn ending as its own failure.

**[B03] Adoption resolves the session's project directory on the server, from the ledger row, rather than taking it from the caller.** [F08] is why it cannot come from the Tripwires row, and [F07] is why it does not have to: the ledger already holds `project_dir` for every session, keyed by id, and that value is the directory claude is actually running in. A caller that had to supply the path would have to know it, which for a trip means the inspection tree — a path the engine owns, mints per landing and deletes. Adoption therefore takes a **session id and nothing else**, which is also what the adoption brief asked for when it said "adoption takes a session id, and knows nothing about wires."

**[B04] "Cardless" becomes a stated property of a session rather than a prefix nobody reads.** [F07] found `TRIPWIRE_CARD_PREFIX` written in two places and read in none, which means the one fact that distinguishes a background session is carried by a string convention with no reader and no guard. Whatever the listing surface turns out to be, the ledger's answer to "does this `card_id` name a deck card?" is computed in one place and named, so the next background agent inherits it rather than inventing a second prefix. This is the whole of what [B07] of the parent meant by "a general capability": not a registry, which the adoption brief ruled out, but one word both sides of the wire agree on.

**[B05] A second card's adoption raises the card that holds the session instead of reporting `session_live_elsewhere`.** [F09] found the refusal already correct and its presentation wrong: the user clicked a row to see a session, and the answer "that session is live elsewhere" is true and useless when the elsewhere is a card three panes over. The refusal stays exactly as it is on the wire — it is the invariant that keeps two processes off one JSONL — and the deck answers it by revealing the holder. Same-card re-adoption remains an ordinary reconnect.

**[B06] The Tripwires row is the first caller and gets nothing beyond the dispatch.** The adoption brief's corollary, carried: the row holds a session id and asks for adoption. Which trip it belongs to, what the tripwire was watching, what the dot means — all of that is the card's own presentation and belongs to Phase 5. This phase touches `tripwires-card.tsx` only enough to make one row activate, and does not begin the card's convention pass.

## Open Questions {#open-questions}

- **What becomes of the trip when its session is adopted?** [B02] decides the engine stops owning the session; it does not decide what the trip row says. The trip cannot stay `running` indefinitely — that holds the tripwire's one-live-run slot, which the parent's [B05] explicitly withdrew as a promise — and it cannot honestly settle `failed`, because nothing failed. [F10] complicates it in both directions: the adopted session still carries the resolution contract and can still settle its own trip, so a user who lets it finish gets the ordinary outcome, while a user who redirects it strands the row. Candidates: a new terminal status meaning *taken over*; a settle with a headline naming the adoption; or leaving it `running` with a deadline that only starts when the card closes. This changes what gets written at the ledger, the engine, and the card, and it is the question the phase most needs answered.

- **Where does the adoption request enter, and which side owns the seat?** The adoption brief's own first bullet, still open after the reading. [F03] argues the deck already has the primitive and only needs the id and a resolved path, which makes adoption a deck-side `fireRestore` over a small server lookup. [B03] and [F06] argue the server knows strictly more — the project dir, whether the entry is live, whether its `card_id` names a real card, and whether a background owner has to be told — which makes a server-answered `adopt_session` CONTROL verb the honest shape. The difference decides whether the handover in [B02] is a supervisor-side fact or a deck-side convention, and it is not settled by anything read here.

- **What does the adopted card bind to once the inspection tree is swept?** [F06] is the problem: a card seated on a diagnosis session shows a project directory that the engine deletes when the trip settles. Whether adoption pins the tree — taking a reference the way a live trip does — or lets the card outlive its directory and say so, changes what the sweep is allowed to do and what the card's masthead can honestly claim.

- **What does the card show at the instant of adoption?** The adoption brief's fifth bullet, unanswered and unchanged: scrolled to the leading question, to the top, or wherever the transcript left off. It is small, but it is the first thing the user sees and the replay bracket's own completion is the only moment it can be decided at.

## Non-goals {#non-goals}

- **Building a replay path, a live-attach reconciliation, or a boundary protocol.** Rejected on [F01] and [F02]: all three exist, ship, and are the ones the deck already uses. A second implementation aimed at background sessions would be the mid-turn-replay work done twice.
- **A read-only transcript view with a "take over" button.** The adoption brief offered this as a possible cheaper first step. It is not cheaper — the composer works the moment the binding exists [F02] [F03] — and it would be a second card body to maintain.
- **A general background-agent registry.** The adoption brief ruled it out and [B04] keeps it out: one named property on a session the ledger already holds, not a new table.
- **The Tripwires card's convention pass.** Phase 5, per the parent [B09]. This phase makes one row activate.
- **The tripwire feed frame and the HTTP API.** Phase 4, per the parent [B08]. Adoption takes a session id, and the row already has one [F08].
- **Holding a pending `QuestionDialog` across adoption.** Deferred by the adoption brief, deferred by the parent, and still deferred.
- **Changing when or why a background session is created.** The caller's business, and for tripwires that was Phases 1 and 2.

---

## Exit {#exit}

**An arc.** The handover is the spine and everything else hangs off it, so the order is: settle what a trip becomes when it is adopted and what the request looks like, because both open questions change the shape of the code below them; teach the engine to stop owning a session whose entry has been re-pointed, so an adopted session survives its owner's wait ending; name "cardless" once, where both sides can read it; route the adoption request and resolve the project dir from the ledger; give the Tripwires row its dispatch and answer a second adoption by revealing the holder; and pin the whole of it with an app-test that adopts a running trip mid-turn, sees the boundary exactly once, and finds the composer live — plus a settled trip that opens onto its full transcript.
