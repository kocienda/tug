# Tug 0.9.4

This release is mostly about charts, diagrams and sharing: the produced images/figures now can be exported out of Tug as pictures and files, diagrams gained two new forms, and clicking a chart mark opens the data behind it.

## Charts and diagrams

- Charts and diagrams can now be copied or shared as a PNG or PDF, not just as JSON — a header's Copy puts a picture on the pasteboard, and a new Share button opens a sheet with Copy, Save… and Share…. Figures shared from a card now open in that same in-deck sheet instead of a system preview window.
- Pressing a chart mark or a Table row that stands for several things (a day of commits, a hotspot directory, a test's run history, and more) opens a report of exactly what it represents — one row per item, each openable the way it's opened anywhere else in Tug, with paging for large sets.
- Clicking into a chart report now holds the panel open until you close it, instead of dismissing on the next click or on Escape, and the mark you pressed is ringed in the chart's accent color so it's clear what the report belongs to.
- Diagrams gained two new forms — **states** (what a thing can be, and what moves it between states) and **tree** (what's inside what) — alongside the existing flow, sequence, layers and stack forms. Every diagram's connectors are now easier to read, with cleaner routing and labels placed on the line they describe.
- A new "Explain Code…" command (also `/explain-code` and `⌃⌘X`) opens a sheet showing the kinds of diagrams Tug can draw from your code, with example requests and sample figures, and a button that drops a ready-to-send request into the composer.
- Chart recipes now count lines more carefully (separating source from tests, data and docs) and the charts about hotspots, commit sizes, and long-running commands all open a report of what's behind their numbers when pressed.
- Good/bad coloring in charts now uses tokens the chart theme owns rather than borrowing the app's general success/danger colors, for better contrast.
- The "Data Visualization" sheet and its command are renamed to **Visualize Data…**, and its Draw button is now labeled "Visualize…".

## Navigation

- Go ▸ Back and Forward now move between the cards you've actually focused, in the order you focused them, crossing into other workspaces when needed. The shortcut is `⌥⇧⌘[` / `⌥⇧⌘]`.
- Switching workspaces and then picking a different card no longer leaves Back stuck on a card you passed through but never actually visited.
- The card a Back or Forward step lands on now briefly flashes, so it's clear where you ended up.

## Other changes

- "Reveal in Finder" is renamed to **Show in Finder** throughout the app.
- A fold in a long test or history row no longer shifts the row sideways by a few pixels when it opens or closes.
- Various small fixes to run offers, settings layout, and session restore timing.
